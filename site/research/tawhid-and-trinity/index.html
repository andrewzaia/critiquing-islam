RESEARCH / CI-022

Tawhid and the Trinity.

Does the Quran address Nicene monotheism, or a different conception of three gods?

Comprehensive analysisMonotheism / Christian doctrineNovember 2026

01 / THE EXACT ISSUE

The exact issue

Islamic Tawhid affirms the absolute oneness of God and repudiates assigning divine status to a partner. Nicene Christianity likewise confesses one God, but distinguishes Father, Son and Holy Spirit as three persons of one undivided divine essence. The existence of this disagreement is clear. What needs proving is whether the Christian position logically entails three separate gods, not whether the Quran condemns three gods. [1] [5]

A polemic that states "Christians worship three gods" misidentifies its formal target. A more sophisticated Muslim objection alleges that three real personal distinctions cannot be reconciled with divine simplicity, unity or independence. That is a genuine philosophical challenge, and it should be evaluated on those terms instead of being replaced by a numerical slogan.

02 / READ THE QURANIC PASSAGES SEPARATELY

Read the Quranic passages separately

Quran 4:171 warns against saying "three", and 5:72-73 rejects divinising Jesus and describing God as the third of three. Quran 5:116 asks Jesus whether he told people to take him and Mary as deities besides God. These verses have overlapping concerns but different grammatical and rhetorical forms. [1] [2] [3]

A claimed contradiction must be precise: 5:73 may be directed at a three-gods conception, a loose formulation of orthodox Trinitarianism or a disputed Christian practice. None of these possibilities automatically supplies the others. In particular, 5:116 does not explicitly define the Trinity as God, Jesus and Mary; it is a question about deification.

03 / THE PRE-ISLAMIC DEFINITION OF THE TRINITY

The pre-Islamic definition of the Trinity

The Nicene Creed of 325 professes belief in one God and identifies the Son as begotten, not made, of the same divine substance as the Father. The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed describes the Holy Spirit as worshipped with Father and Son. These formal texts are centuries earlier than Islam and contradict the assertion that orthodox Christianity officially believed in three independent gods. [5] [6]

That documentary point has limits. A creed records what an institutional tradition taught, not what every community believed or whether its assertions are true. Nevertheless, if the question is what Trinitarian doctrine means, its pre-Islamic primary formulations are the proper starting point. A critic cannot simply substitute a doctrine the creed denies.

04 / THE MARY ALLEGATION

The Mary allegation

Quran 5:116 is commonly cited as evidence that the Quran mistook Mary for the third person of the Trinity. The wording does not say that. It criticises taking Jesus and Mary as deities beside God; it does not name the Holy Spirit or claim that Christians wrote Mary into the Nicene Creed. Even Muslim explanations of this verse distinguish the question of Mary's veneration from the formal Trinity. [3] [10]

The stronger question is historical: what evidence exists for particular seventh-century communities treating Mary as a goddess, rather than venerating her as the mother of Jesus? Specific communities, dates, practices and documentary sources are needed. Reverence and worship are not automatically identical, and the behaviour of a minority cannot be projected onto all Christians without evidence.

05 / THE EARLIEST CHRISTIAN TRIADIC EVIDENCE

The earliest Christian triadic evidence

Matthew 28:19 names Father, Son and Holy Spirit together in baptismal instruction; 2 Corinthians 13:14 offers a triadic blessing; and 1 Corinthians 8:4-6 speaks of one God and one Lord Jesus. Their dates and composition histories are open to normal textual analysis, but these writings are not inventions of the fourth-century council or of post-Islamic Christianity. [7] [8] [9]

Their existence also does not prove that the full later formula of one essence and three persons was verbally fixed in the first century. The research question is one of doctrinal development: which early claims led to which later definitions, and what changed? A historically responsible critique must admit both continuity and development.

06 / IS THE ARITHMETIC OBJECTION VALID?

Is the arithmetic objection valid?

Saying "one plus one plus one equals three" treats the persons as three instances of the same kind of being. Nicene doctrine denies that premise. Its model distinguishes what God is from who Father, Son and Spirit are. The basic arithmetical objection therefore does not by itself demonstrate an internal contradiction.

But the Christian response does not end the argument. What exactly counts as a person? Can three distinct personal relations be real without introducing multiple agents, dependencies or components? If the defender redefines "person" whenever an objection arises, the account risks obscurity. Both the critic and defender must articulate a stable metaphysical model.

07 / THE CHALLENGE FROM DIVINE SIMPLICITY

The challenge from divine simplicity

A sophisticated Islamic argument contends that an absolutely simple God cannot include real internal relational distinctions. That inference needs demonstration. The proposition that divine simplicity is incompatible with the Trinity is not established merely by defining simplicity as the absence of Trinitarian relations.

Conversely, a Christian may argue that eternal relations are consistent with the unity of essence, but must clarify how a relation can distinguish persons without adding separable substances or attributes. The difficulty is substantive, not rhetorical. This is where the best interfaith philosophical debate belongs.

08 / JESUS' HUMAN LIFE IS NOT A COMPLETE REFUTATION

Jesus' human life is not a complete refutation

Quran 3:59 likens the creation of Jesus to Adam's creation and blocks an inference from unusual birth alone to divinity. This works against the narrow argument that a virgin birth logically proves Godhood. But the Christian incarnation claim rests instead on the identity of a pre-existent Word, articulated in John 1, not solely on birth circumstances. [11] [12]

Jesus' hunger, prayer and limitations are real challenges to an unqualified view of divine omnipotence in a human life. Classical Christianity answers by distinguishing human and divine natures. Whether that distinction is coherent requires further analysis; merely citing hunger does not refute a doctrine that expressly includes genuine humanity.

09 / WHAT IS THE STATUS OF QURAN 112?

What is the status of Quran 112?

Quran 112 expresses a theological premise about God's absolute oneness and non-begetting. It establishes the Islamic stance with exceptional clarity. It does not independently verify the metaphysical impossibility of divine persons, because that is the contested proposition itself. [4]

The parallel standard applies to Christianity: quoting the Nicene Creed establishes what Christianity teaches, not that God is triune. An evidence-led comparison must distinguish scripture as evidence of a belief from independent philosophical or historical reasons for accepting the belief.

10 / REQUIREMENTS FOR A DEFENSIBLE CRITIQUE

Requirements for a defensible critique

A serious refutation must first define the orthodox position from its primary sources, then establish the specific meaning of the Quranic passage under examination. It should distinguish three-gods arguments, criticism of Mary's worship and criticism of one-essence Trinitarianism. Those are not identical hypotheses.

The next layer requires historical evidence for any alleged worshipping sect, explanation of the development from New Testament texts to councils, and an explicit philosophical account of why one essence cannot sustain three persons. If the Quran's divine authority is the deciding premise, that authority must be defended independently rather than assumed.

11 / CONCLUSION

Conclusion

The documentary mismatch is real where a critic attacks a supposed three-gods creed: the Nicene texts explicitly teach one God. Quran 5:116 does not, by its wording alone, prove that the Quran mistook Mary for the Holy Spirit. The remaining question of whether the Trinity is coherent and true cannot be settled by a caricature or by repeating either tradition's statement of faith.

For more, compare Quran 5:116 and Trinitarian doctrine, Jesus' divinity, and the Son of God investigation.
