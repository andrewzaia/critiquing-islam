01 / HOW THESE TEN CLAIMS ARE TESTED

How these ten claims are tested

SCOPE OF THE AUDIT

This guide brings ten Islamic claims into one referenced comparison, linking to longer research where available. Metaphysical doctrines are not automatically refuted by historical disagreements. The strongest tests ask whether a claim describes its opponent accurately, follows from its cited text and survives independent historical or textual checks.

For each subject we separate the claim, the strongest counter-reading, and the additional proof needed. The standard is symmetric: Christian or Muslim statements about supernatural reality cannot be made historically certain merely by quoting their own scripture. An early text establishes an early belief; its truth needs a further argument.

02 / 1. TAWHID AND THE TRINITY

1. Tawhid and the Trinity

Islamic position. Quran 4:171 prohibits saying 'three'; Quran 112 expresses radical divine oneness; 5:72-73 criticises attributing divinity to Christ and a formulation of 'third of three'. [1] [2] [6]

Critical distinction. Nicene Christianity also asserts one God, not three separate gods. Its claim is one divine being in three distinct persons. A critique based on 'Christians worship three gods' must show that this accurately represents its target; a comparison with 5:116 must distinguish criticism of Marian veneration from a supposed definition of the Trinity. [3]

Proof requirements. Identify which doctrine is denied, whether 5:73 is intended to describe orthodox Trinitarian metaphysics or a popular formulation, and whether philosophical objections to personal distinctions actually establish logical contradiction. A conflict of theological premises is not, by itself, an historical refutation. Read the dedicated Trinity investigation.

03 / 2. GOD HAVING A SON: BIOLOGICAL OR ETERNAL?

2. God having a Son: biological or eternal?

Islamic position. Quran 19:35 says it is unfitting for God to take a son and 112:3 denies divine begetting. This is an unequivocal theological rejection. [4] [2]

Central challenge. Classical Christianity's 'only-begotten Son' is not the assertion that God physically reproduced with Mary. The Nicene Creed explicitly describes eternal begetting, not creation or sexual reproduction; John 1 also links sonship to the pre-existent Word. Polemic that defeats only a biological caricature fails to engage the asserted doctrine. [3] [5]

Proof requirements. Determine whether a Quranic rejection targets all forms of divine sonship or a culturally specific account; compare the earliest Christian texts rather than a modern simplification. The two traditions truly disagree about divine identity, but proving which account is true needs arguments additional to their respective self-descriptions.

04 / 3. JESUS AS PROPHET AND MESSIAH, RATHER THAN DIVINE

3. Jesus as prophet and Messiah, rather than divine

Islamic position. Quran 5:75 treats the Messiah as a messenger who ate food, 4:171 calls him God's messenger, and 3:45 calls him the Messiah. Quran 19:19 calls him a pure boy, but the blanket claim 'the Quran expressly says Jesus never sinned at any point' should not be presented without further exegesis. [6] [1] [8]

Historical tension. The early Christian record already contains elevated claims about Jesus, including Philippians 2:5-11, long before Muhammad. That dates Christian belief but does not establish its truth. The later Quranic correction needs its own warrant if it is advanced as history rather than confession. [7]

Proof requirements. Identify first-century teachings, assess the chronology and textual witnesses, distinguish Jesus' historically reconstructable claims from later belief, and test the Quran's source of information independently. Read the extended Christology study and the study of Jesus' religious identity.

05 / 4. CRUCIFIXION, APPARENT DEATH AND SUBSTITUTION

4. Crucifixion, apparent death and substitution

Islamic position. Quran 4:157-158 denies that the speakers killed or crucified Jesus and says the matter was made to appear so. The verse does not name a substitute; substitution stories occur in later interpretations and differ among themselves. [9]

Historical challenge. Crucifixion is part of early Pauline testimony and the Gospel tradition; Tacitus also refers to an execution under Pilate. These witnesses have different degrees of proximity and independence and cannot simply be counted as independent eyewitnesses. [19] [10]

Proof requirements. A specific substitute hypothesis must identify the source, proposed person and date, explain why contrary accounts arose early, and distinguish a theological assertion from contemporary attestation. Minority readings of 4:157 do not deny a crucifixion. Read the 17-section historical investigation.

06 / 5. TAHRIF: WHAT EXACTLY WAS CORRUPTED?

5. Tahrif: what exactly was corrupted?

Islamic position. Quran 5:43-48 simultaneously affirms earlier divine guidance and gives the Quran a governing role. Charges of falsification exist elsewhere, but accusations of altered text, misleading interpretation and concealment should not be collapsed into one universal allegation. [11] [28]

Historical challenge. Pre-Islamic biblical codices make sweeping claims of post-Islamic rewriting untenable for passages actually attested in them. They do not prove that earlier texts were variant-free. [12]

Proof requirements. Specify the allegedly altered verse, mechanism, approximate date, manuscript evidence and competing explanation. Most importantly, do not use the Quran's authority as the sole evidence of the Quran's authority. Read the earlier-scripture study and Al-Muhaymin examined in Arabic.

07 / 6. THE FINAL, UNALTERED QURAN

6. The final, unaltered Quran

Islamic position. Quran 15:9 expresses a divine protection claim, and the traditional narrative treats the Quran as final and preserved. [13]

Historical challenge. Sahih al-Bukhari 4987 reports Uthman's standardisation and disposal of other written materials. Surviving manuscripts support substantial early textual stability while showing why the word 'perfect' needs precise definition. A shared early consonantal archetype is not the same claim as every historical recitation and manuscript being identical. [14]

Proof requirements. Separate preservation of core text, consonantal skeleton, approved readings, verse arrangement and total word-for-word identity. Evaluate manuscripts, variant readings and collection reports under the same standard applied to biblical transmission. Read the manuscript investigation and the Uthman analysis.

08 / 7. MUHAMMAD AS SEAL OF THE PROPHETS

7. Muhammad as Seal of the Prophets

Islamic position. Quran 33:40 calls Muhammad khatam al-nabiyyin, traditionally understood as final prophet. The reported final-brick analogy makes the classical reading clear. [15] [16]

Critical distinction. Establishing that the Quran and later tradition teach finality does not independently establish that God closed revelation. Some interpretive debate concerns the precise force of seal, but it would be inaccurate to conceal the dominant finality reading.

Proof requirements. Test the morphology in context, the relevant hadith transmission, whether 'prophet' and 'messenger' are distinguished consistently, and the claimed credentials of Muhammad. If finality is defended solely by referring to Muhammad's revelation, the argument presupposes the very prophethood under examination. Compare claimed earlier-scripture predictions and Ahmad and the Paraclete.

09 / 8. ORIGINAL SIN, RESPONSIBILITY AND ATONEMENT

8. Original sin, responsibility and atonement

Islamic position. Quran 6:164 says nobody bears another's moral burden; Quran 2:37 narrates Adam's repentance. Islam generally does not frame humanity as personally guilty for Adam's act and rejects the Christian claim that Christ died as a sin-atoning sacrifice. [17] [26]

Critical distinction. Christian traditions do not all teach inherited personal culpability in the same way. Catholic and Eastern Orthodox accounts distinguish the fallen human condition from a newborn's personal act. Romans 5 compares Adam with Christ; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 makes death for sins and resurrection a very early Christian proclamation. [18] [19]

Proof requirements. State exactly which Christian doctrine is targeted, distinguish guilt, mortality and human inclination, and test whether substitutionary atonement necessarily violates personal accountability or involves a voluntary representative. The two traditions' moral frameworks compete; merely declaring the other unjust does not settle the metaphysical question.

10 / 9. FAITH, WORKS, GRACE AND DIVINE MERCY

9. Faith, works, grace and divine mercy

Islamic position. Quranic salvation discourse includes faith, righteous action, repentance, divine judgement and mercy. Quran 2:62 and 3:85 generate interpretive questions about membership and revelation. [20]

Essential corrective. 'Islam teaches salvation earned by works alone' is false as a general description. In Bukhari 5673 Muhammad is reported to say that no one's deeds suffice for Paradise without God's favour and mercy. Christian traditions also differ on justification, grace and the relation of faith to works. [21]

Proof requirements. Define what is necessary, sufficient or evidentiary for salvation, whether salvation promises extend to non-Muslims and what authentic reports say about mercy. An apparent contradiction must survive the strongest coherent contextual reading. Read the salvation-text study.

11 / 10. JESUS' RETURN AND ISLAMIC ESCHATOLOGY

10. Jesus' return and Islamic eschatology

Islamic position. Quran 4:159 and 43:61 are read by many exegetes as related to Jesus' return, but their exegesis is contested. Much of the detailed picture - Jesus descending, breaking the cross and judging under Islamic law - derives from hadith, especially Bukhari 3448 and Muslim 155a. [22] [23] [24]

Christian contrast. Matthew 25 presents the Son of Man returning in glory to judge the nations. The Nicene Creed likewise confesses Christ returning to judge the living and the dead. This is a substantive disagreement over Jesus' identity and authority, not merely over the calendar of the end times. [25] [3]

Proof requirements. Separate explicit Quranic wording from hadith and commentary; establish which traditions are invoked and their dating; compare pre-Islamic Christian evidence; and recognise that an unfulfilled future prediction cannot presently be checked by direct historical observation. The responsible verdict is a documented theological divergence, not a proven future event.

12 / WHICH CONCLUSIONS ARE DEFENSIBLE?

Which conclusions are defensible?

Textually verified: the Quran denies divine sonship, rejects the Trinitarian formulation as understood by Muslim exegetes, describes Muhammad as seal of the prophets, and presents a different account of Jesus' death from dominant early Christianity.

Historically testable: the dates of Christian claims about Jesus, the manuscript basis for alleged corruption, Quranic standardisation, the formation of traditions and whether claimed prophecies are independently attested.

Not established by textual quotation alone: God's metaphysical nature, the truth of Muhammad's prophetic office, miraculous preservation, the actual mechanism of an alleged crucifixion substitute, or the future return of Jesus.

Next-layer research priorities: dedicated deep dives into divine sonship (2), prophetic finality (7), original sin and atonement (8), and the hadith basis for the second coming (10). The present ten-part study supplies source-based initial coverage and explicit unanswered questions rather than mislabelling every topic 'fully proven'.
