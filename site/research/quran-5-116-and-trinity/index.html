RESEARCH / CI-007

Does the Quran describe the Trinity accurately?

The Quran rebukes deifying Jesus and Mary. How closely does that criticism correspond to the central Christian creed?

Published analysisPrimary-text examinationNovember 2026

01 / DEFINE THE QUESTION

Mary appears in a condemnation of deification

TEXTUAL FINDING

Quran 5:116 describes Jesus and Mary being taken as deities besides God. That is not the Trinity defined by the Nicene Creed. The verse does not explicitly say that Mary is a member of the Christian Trinity, so this mismatch must not be inflated into a claim that the Quran formally defines the Trinity as God, Jesus and Mary.

A fair test separates two propositions: that the Quran condemns the deification of Mary and Jesus, and that it accurately represents orthodox Christianity's account of the Trinity. Those propositions are not the same.

02 / QURANIC PASSAGES

What do 5:116, 5:73 and 4:171 actually say?

In Quran 5:116, God asks Jesus whether he instructed people to take him and his mother as deities besides God. The verse presents Jesus rejecting such a claim. [1] Quran 5:73 condemns those who describe God as a third of three, and 4:171 warns the People of the Book against saying three. [2] [3]

Quran 5:75 identifies Jesus as a messenger and points out that Jesus and Mary ate food. [4] This fits the polemical emphasis on their humanity. These passages together show a sustained rejection of divine status being accorded to Jesus, and 5:116 extends that rejection to Mary.

None of the cited Quranic sentences explicitly provides the standard creedal list Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Equally, 5:116 does not state, in those words, that the Trinity consists of Mary, Jesus and God.

03 / THE PRIMARY CHRISTIAN CREED

What does Trinitarian Christianity profess?

The Nicene Creed professes one God, identifies Jesus Christ as the Son and confesses the Holy Spirit as Lord. The three persons named in traditional Trinitarian doctrine are the Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Mary is described as the woman from whom Christ was born, not as a divine person. [5]

In Luke 1:46-47, Mary directs praise to God rather than describing herself as a deity. [6] Catholic and Orthodox traditions give Mary exceptional honour, but honouring or seeking the intercession of a saint is not identical to declaring that saint to be God. The distinction between veneration and worship matters for evaluating a doctrinal representation.

What the Creed actually establishes

It establishes the normative Trinitarian formulation, not that every historical Christian behaved consistently with it. An accusation against a particular form of practice therefore needs evidence of that practice.

04 / INTERPRETIVE OPTIONS

A criticism of practice, or a misunderstanding of doctrine?

One reading takes 5:116 as targeting people who treated Jesus and Mary as divine in practice, regardless of their formal creed. Under that reading it need not be a technical definition of the Trinity at all. The narrower interpretation is textually possible because the verse asks about making them deities; it does not use the word Trinity. [1]

A different reading connects 5:116 with the adjacent Quranic criticism of those who say three, arguing that the text misunderstands the people it is addressing. The juxtaposition is relevant, but the passages do not explicitly equate Mary's deification with the Christian three persons. That stronger identification requires evidence from interpretation and historical context, not just repetition of 5:116.

The resulting question is evidentiary: if the criticism aims at Christianity's central creed, it misses that creed. If it targets a particular Marian devotion thought to cross into worship, the historical target must be identified and demonstrated.

05 / CONCLUSION

The mismatch is real; the identification must be precise

The Christian Trinity does not consist of God, Jesus and Mary. The Nicene Creed clearly identifies Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Quran 5:116 instead condemns the taking of Jesus and Mary as deities, an accusation that does not describe the orthodox creed. [1] [5]

It is therefore incorrect to claim that the deification of Mary is a feature of standard Trinitarian teaching. It would also be incorrect to assert, solely from 5:116, that the Quran explicitly defines the Trinity as a three-person combination including Mary. The argument's strength lies in identifying exactly what the Quran criticises and testing whether the claimed Christian target fits.

Verdict: The claimed portrayal fails as a description of Nicene Christianity. If Quran 5:116 is offered as an account of orthodox Trinitarian doctrine, it does not match the Creed, which identifies Father, Son and Holy Spirit, not Mary. Because 5:116 does not explicitly define the Trinity, the broader claim that the Quran states Mary belongs to it remains unproven.
