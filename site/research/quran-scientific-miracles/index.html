01 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

What would qualify as a scientific miracle?

THE CLAIM

Scientific-miracle arguments assert that the Quran contains information unavailable to its original audience and subsequently confirmed by scientific research. [1] [3] A broad compatibility with a later finding is not the same as a unique prediction.

This investigation distinguishes what the Quran actually says, what modern observations establish and whether a connection between them supports a supernatural inference. A scientific observation is evidence for the observation, not automatically for every scripture later claimed to anticipate it. The appropriate test must be fixed before favourable examples are selected.

02 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Original wording, specificity and independence are required

A substantive predictive claim needs wording that supports the relevant meaning in its Arabic context, a sufficiently precise proposition to distinguish it from alternative accounts, and an independently verified phenomenon. It should explain why ordinary observation, pre-existing knowledge or common metaphor does not account for the statement. [2] [4] [6]

If an interpretation is chosen only after a discovery, its evidential value is much lower than a precise interpretation demonstrably made before that discovery. Conversely, scientific testing must not claim that every metaphor is a literal scientific error. A strong critique preserves the same interpretative standard whichever result it produces.

03 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Quran 21:30 does not uniquely state the Big Bang model

Quran 21:30 describes heavens and earth as joined or closed together before being separated. Several English translations differ in nuance, but none provides a mathematical model, a primordial temperature or an observable cosmological parameter. [1] [2] The statement is a creation description, not an independent measurement of the early universe.

The Big Bang model concerns the development of the universe from an earlier hot dense condition, whereas Earth itself formed much later. NASA's account emphasises the expansion of space rather than a conventional explosion into an already existing void. [6] Mapping the pair of heaven and earth onto these physical processes requires decisions not supplied by the verse.

04 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Scientific agreement cannot be supplied retrospectively

A poetic joining and separation may resemble a simplified modern retelling of cosmic origins. That resemblance is meaningful only if the verse can exclude other plausible interpretations without having the modern result imported into it. The exact early-universe sequence and supporting observations are absent from the claim. [1] [6]

The fact of cosmic expansion is supported by measured light and distance. It is not established by attaching a modern explanation to a broad ancient description. A persuasive prediction would identify a discriminating feature before the scientific observation became known. [5]

05 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Quran 51:47 permits breadth but not a unique Hubble prediction

Quran 51:47 says the heaven was built with power and uses the emphatic participle lamusiuna, from a root associated with extent and capacity. Translations vary between making vast, extending and possessing abundance or power. [3] [4] This linguistic range needs to be acknowledged rather than hidden.

Even the strongest translation in terms of expansion lacks galaxies, redshift, a change in scale over time or a quantitative relationship between distance and recession. NASA documents such expansion through observations of light from galaxies, independently of this passage. [5] [15] Thus compatibility with expansion does not prove prediction of the modern physical model.

06 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Granting the strongest translation still leaves the proof gap

Suppose the verse means that God expands the heaven. This is closer to current cosmological language than a reading confined to divine power. Yet the same words can describe creating a vast sky, and the text does not identify the measurable process that modern astronomers investigate. [3] [4]

The advocate must show why a physical, ongoing, time-dependent interpretation was required by the historical Arabic rather than selected after discovery. The critic need not insist that an expansion reading is linguistically impossible; only that it is not uniquely established as extraordinary foreknowledge. [15]

07 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Smoke and seven heavens lack a demonstrated physical mapping

Quran 41:9-12 describes earthly creation, a sky in a smoke-like condition, and seven heavens. The word rendered smoke or vapour is not a measured property of primordial plasma, and the passage supplies neither a detailed chronology nor modern physical conditions. [7] [8]

Claiming this passage predicted modern astrophysics requires specifying why the description maps to the observed universe and what empirical structure corresponds to the seven heavens. The NASA sources verifying expansion do not establish the seven heavens as a physical scientific classification. [5] [6]

08 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Quran 23:12-14 supplies a sequence, not modern embryology

The Quran describes reproductive material, a clinging form, a lump, bones and the clothing of bones with flesh. The sequence is present in the Arabic grammatical analysis, although key terms have a translation range. [9] [10] The description does not specify cells, germ layers or the timing of organogenesis.

OpenStax and UNSW document tissue differentiation in which developing skeletal and muscular systems arise through related embryonic processes. A rigid claim that complete bones form before any relevant flesh or muscle develops is not an accurate technical description. A broader poetic reading may be defensible but cannot also claim the precision of modern developmental science. [11] [12]

09 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Translation flexibility cannot rescue a technical claim automatically

A critic should not assume the term for a clinging form must always signify a literal blood clot. That would exclude linguistic possibilities without warrant. [10] But it is equally unjustified to insist a term encodes a microscopic modern stage and then invoke broad metaphor whenever a specific ordering does not match observation.

Identifying the intended degree of precision before evaluating evidence is essential. The text offers a recognisable account of human development, yet that alone is not evidence that unavailable embryological mechanisms were anticipated. [9] [11] [12]

10 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Quran 86:5-7 poses a distinct anatomical question

The Quran describes the human as created from emitted or gushing fluid coming from between the backbone and ribs. The location varies slightly among English translations, but the spatial wording is part of the passage and cannot simply be omitted. [13] [14]

Defences may refer to semen, reproductive origins during embryonic development or another fluid. These are different anatomical claims, each needing an explicit linguistic justification and comparison with the actual biological process. Moving between them after an objection is not verification. The text does not independently settle the modern interpretation advertised on its behalf.

11 / TEXT AND EVIDENCE

Water and living things are a real but non-unique correspondence

Quran 21:30 associates every living thing with water. That is broadly consistent with terrestrial biology and should not be denied simply because it is a Quranic claim. [1] The dependence of animals, plants and people on water, however, is also accessible to ordinary observation. The correspondence does not, by itself, show inaccessible knowledge.

A scientific miracle case would need a specific, unexpected prediction and evidence that the original audience could not infer it by familiar experience. Agreement with an elementary observation is weaker than that evidentiary threshold.

12 / QURAN 21:30 IN ARABIC: THE HEAVENS AND EARTH ARE THE PAIRED SUBJECT

Quran 21:30 in Arabic: the heavens and earth are the paired subject

The Quranic Arabic Corpus parses al-samawati wa al-arda as the heavens and the earth, followed by the feminine dual kanata, they were, the predicate ratqan, joined or closed, and fa-fataqnahuma, then we parted the two. The dual suffix points back to the paired heaven and earth, not to a scientifically specified early-universe state. The grammar describes separation but supplies no density, temperature, date, expansion law or mechanism. [19]

This observation is neither a proof of modern cosmology nor a demonstration that the verse is scientifically erroneous. Creation poetry can express the division of a primordial unity and still be a meaningful religious claim. Its evidential weakness is lack of specificity: a broad split could refer to several physical or literary processes, and the text does not choose the hot Big Bang mechanism over those alternatives before modern science is supplied.

The distinction matters historically. Genesis 1:6-10, a pre-Islamic text, already presents primordial waters being separated and dry land appearing. The passages are not verbally identical, nor does the parallel establish that the Quran author copied Genesis through a specific intermediary. It does demonstrate that cosmic separation imagery predates Islam, making a bare declaration that a joined-and-separated world was inconceivable earlier untenable. [20]

Finding: the original Arabic supports a paired separation image. It does not uniquely express cosmic inflation, the beginning of spacetime, or the modern sequence by which Earth and the observable galaxies developed.

13 / THE SCIENTIFIC BIG BANG MODEL HAS DISCRIMINATING OBSERVATIONS

The scientific Big Bang model has discriminating observations

Modern cosmology is not merely the idea that once-connected things became separated. NASA describes observational support including redshift of distant galaxies, the cosmic microwave background and the evolution of structure from a hot, dense early universe. Maps from WMAP measure tiny anisotropies in the microwave background radiation left from a much earlier epoch. Those observations distinguish the model from numerous possible ancient creation accounts. [5] [6] [34]

Quran 21:30 contains no directly comparable evidence, such as a microwave relic, a quantitative relation between distance and recession, primordial light-element ratios or a testable temperature. The Earth is explicitly named in the verse, but the planet formed long after the initial conditions described by the Big Bang model. Mapping Earth directly onto the earliest cosmic state risks an anachronism; reinterpreting it as undifferentiated matter avoids that objection only by adding a meaning that must be defended from the Arabic.

A reader can interpret the passage as compatible with God's creative act and with scientific discovery. Compatibility is a lower threshold than foreknowledge. The required stronger proposition is that the wording, in its historical setting, singled out a discovery that ordinary available ideas could not have generated, before that discovery was independently made. A general dual separation fails this level of discrimination even if no physical impossibility is proved.

14 / QURAN 51:47: THE FORM IV PARTICIPLE DOES NOT UNIQUELY ENCODE EXPANSION

Quran 51:47: the form IV participle does not uniquely encode expansion

The debated word in Quran 51:47 is la-musi'una, a prefixed emphatic particle followed by a masculine plural form IV active participle from the root w-s-a, relating to wideness, capacity or abundance. The Quranic Arabic Corpus glosses it as expanders. It is legitimate to consider an expanding-heaven interpretation; denying that linguistic possibility in advance would weaken the critique. [4]

Yet the same participial lemma occurs in Quran 2:236, where al-musi'i identifies a man of means, contrasted with one whose resources are narrow. This actual Quranic usage demonstrates the range of capability or abundance, not an exclusively astronomical technical meaning. It also exposes why the reader cannot mechanically convert a broad root into a time-dependent expansion law. The surrounding clause in 51:47 says God built heaven with strength and includes no explicit observational quantity. [21] [3]

Even if one grants the strongest translation, "we are expanding it", the modern cosmological claim contains testable spacetime evolution, recession relationships and observational measurements. The verse contains none of these. Moreover, an active participle is not by itself a present continuous scientific description of how a scale factor changes with cosmic time. The miracle proponent must show that the expansion interpretation was uniquely warranted before modern redshift observations, not simply available after they became known. [15]

Finding: an expansion reading is linguistically possible, and a physical expansion of space is established by astronomy. The identification of the two as supernatural foreknowledge is an additional inference which lacks the required original-language and historical specificity.

15 / QURAN 41:11: SMOKE IS NOT A TECHNICAL DESCRIPTION OF PRIMORDIAL PLASMA

Quran 41:11: smoke is not a technical description of primordial plasma

Quran 41:11 describes the heaven as dukhan, smoke, while it is being addressed in a creation discourse that also discusses the earth. Multiple translations use smoke or vapour. In the next verse the heavens are made seven in number. These images are genuine parts of the Quranic narrative and should not be silently replaced with a modern diagram of plasma recombination. [7] [8] [22]

Ionised plasma in the hot early universe, its subsequent cooling, and the release of the cosmic microwave background are specific physical phenomena; calling them a type of smoke is an analogy, not a measurement. Ordinary smoke involves particles and gases produced by burning or other processes, whereas the early-universe plasma has different conditions and mechanisms. A broad visual resemblance does not establish detailed foreknowledge of the latter. [34]

There is also a structural problem for claims of exact scientific mapping. The passage places seven heavens in its account, but modern astronomy does not identify seven physical layers of the observable universe that correspond in any specific testable way to the Quran's seven heavens. One may regard the number as a theological category or cosmological image; doing so gives up the claim that the whole account is a precise modern scientific description.

A serious reconciliation can treat the language as imagery accessible to its original listeners. It need not label all such imagery a scientific error. But a metaphor sufficiently flexible to fit several cosmic processes cannot simultaneously be presented as a uniquely accurate, inaccessible scientific prediction without additional dated interpretive evidence.

16 / GALEN PROVIDES A PRE-ISLAMIC ACCOUNT OF STAGED FETAL DEVELOPMENT

Galen provides a pre-Islamic account of staged fetal development

The *Corpus Coranicum* edition presents a passage from Galen's De Semine, written centuries before Islam, that distinguishes several developmental periods. The cited Greek medical author moves from semen, to material described as filled with blood and taking on flesh-like form, to outlines of principal organs and limbs, and finally to recognisable differentiation. The digital *Corpus* gives a modern translation and an exact scholarly edition reference. A separate Greek edition of Galen's On the Formation of the Foetus preserves extensive ancient discussion of how embryonic development could be studied and described. [23] [24]

This is positive historical evidence against the assertion that an ordinary premodern audience could possess no concept of successive fetal stages. Greek physicians had already discussed such stages by the second century AD. The accounts are not identical: Galen elaborates on anatomy and dissection in ways the Quran does not, and some Quranic terms or emphases differ. A responsible argument therefore identifies the shared motif without declaring a verbatim quotation or a proven line of transmission to Muhammad.

The chronologies must be separated. Galen's composition is pre-Islamic, while the modern web edition and surviving manuscripts are later witnesses to that ancient work. The existence of an earlier articulated medical theory weakens a universal originality claim; it does not establish a particular Syriac or Arabic intermediary into the Hijaz. Demonstrating literary dependence would require a historically credible contact route and sufficiently distinctive correspondences.

Historical verdict: staged embryo descriptions were not historically unavailable before Islam. Their existence is compatible with independent observation, inherited medical knowledge or some form of cultural transmission, none of which is individually proved by the parallel alone.

17 / QURAN 23:14 AND 76:2: DISTINGUISH MORPHOLOGY FROM EMBRYOLOGICAL TIMING

Quran 23:14 and 76:2: distinguish morphology from embryological timing

Quran 23:14 describes a sequence from nutfah to alaqah, then mudghah, followed by bones and a clothing of bones with flesh. The verbs make successive stages part of the passage. Yet the terms refer to ordinary objects or forms that can be compared to reproductive matter, something clinging or clot-like, and a chewed lump. Translating them as uniquely modern cellular or organ stages already adds a biological classification not encoded in the terms themselves. [9] [10]

Quran 76:2 describes a nutfah amshaj, usually rendered a mixed drop, and offers a stronger alternative to the accusation that the Quran necessarily imagines only a male contribution to reproduction. A plausible translation can include a mixture without specifying genetic recombination or the anatomy of a sperm cell and an ovum. Some modern translations directly name sperm and egg, but that detail needs to be distinguished from the underlying Arabic words. [25]

Modern developmental biology describes interacting embryonic processes, including concurrent differentiation of skeletal precursors and muscle lineages. It does not support a simple universal sequence in which a complete articulated skeleton is finished before flesh begins to form. Conversely, a broad metaphor saying an embryo gains structure then visible tissue is less obviously in conflict with biology. It is also much weaker as a claim of precise technical foreknowledge. [11] [12]

These two interpretive positions should not be swapped opportunistically. If the argument depends on exact anatomical staging, it must withstand modern developmental comparisons and evidence about the original Arabic. If it relies on poetic stages, the prediction is too general to establish an otherwise inaccessible microscopic discovery. Both legitimate alternatives can be examined without introducing false absolutes about every word.

18 / QURAN 86:6-7 AND THE DOCUMENTED ORIGIN OF REPRODUCTIVE FLUID

Quran 86:6-7 and the documented origin of reproductive fluid

Quran 86:6-7 describes a person as created from a spurting fluid emerging from between al-sulb and al-tara'ib, conventionally represented as backbone and ribs or chest bones. The Quranic Arabic Corpus records the relevant original wording, while translations vary over whether the region is specifically anatomical or poetically associated with the parent body. [13] [14] [27]

Modern male reproductive anatomy is directly documented: sperm develop in the testes, mature in the epididymis, pass through the vas deferens, and are combined with secretions from the seminal vesicles, prostate and other glands before ejaculation. A literal claim that the ejaculated semen originates in an area between the backbone and ribs therefore does not match the established location of its production and delivery structures. [26]

The strongest alternatives need to be tested separately. One treats the passage as describing the contribution of both male and female anatomy; another says it refers to embryonic migration of the male gonads; another takes backbone and ribs as a metaphor for the body's supporting framework. These may be proposed, but each has its own difficulties. Embryonic location is not the same as the anatomical source of adult ejaculate, and a metaphorical body reference abandons a precise predictive claim about the modern reproductive tract.

Text-first verdict: the Arabic spatial phrase is real, and a literal modern semen-source interpretation conflicts with observed anatomy. Other readings may prevent a categorical scientific-error conclusion, but they do not provide evidence that the verse uniquely anticipated modern reproductive physiology.

19 / QURAN 16:15 AND THE CLAIM THAT MOUNTAINS PREVENT GROUND MOTION

Quran 16:15 and the claim that mountains prevent ground motion

Quran 16:15 says God set rawasiya, firm or firmly set mountains, in the earth an tamida bikum, lest it should sway or shake with you. The Arabic Corpus parses tamida as a third-person feminine singular imperfect verb, whose subject is the earth. The connection between mountains and an earth not shaking or swaying is part of the wording, not a late commentator's invention. [28]

The scientifically strong claim sometimes offered is that mountain ranges physically prevent earthquakes or stabilise tectonic plates. The geological data point in a different direction. The US Geological Survey describes the Himalayas as a product of colliding continental plates; the British Geological Survey explains that continental collisions form mountains, fold belts and reverse faults associated with seismic activity. Mountain belts commonly mark active tectonic processes rather than structures that halt the motion of the earth's crust. [29]

Care is required in interpretation: tamida can denote swaying or instability and need not be a modern technical term for earthquakes. The verse may present a phenomenological image of an inhabitable stable ground rather than a strict prediction that tremors never occur in mountainous regions. That broader reading is possible but does not provide a scientific discovery about earthquakes. A literal worldwide stabilisation theory would need measurable evidence that mountains cause the asserted suppression of movement, not merely a metaphor comparing geological masses to supports.

Verdict: the mountain-stability language exists and may carry ordinary observational meaning. The specific modern claim that mountains secure the crust against tectonic motion is not established and is inconsistent with the standard account of active mountain building.

20 / QURAN 78:6-7: MOUNTAINS ARE CALLED PEGS, NOT SEISMOMETERS

Quran 78:6-7: mountains are called pegs, not seismometers

Quran 78:6-7 asks whether God made the earth a resting place and mountains awtadan, stakes or pegs. The Quranic Arabic Corpus parses the term as a plural accusative noun and confirms the image in its immediate rhetorical setting. It does not state the density of the crust, the location of particular roots, the rate of plate motion or a relationship between mountain height and depth. [30]

The analogy has an understandable everyday basis: a peg is driven into something and can secure a tent or other structure. Some mountain ranges have deep crustal roots, an observed property of isostatic equilibrium. This can make a peg image appear superficially compatible with geology. But isostatic roots do not prove that mountains were installed to prevent earthquakes, and the passage does not specify isostasy in the first place. It would be an error to deny genuine roots where they exist, just as it would be an error to turn the analogy into a detailed theory of plate tectonics. [29]

Reading Quran 78:7 together with 16:15 can strengthen the religious image of mountains stabilising a habitable world. It cannot, without new evidence, establish the causal mechanism a scientific-miracle argument needs. The scientific test would require quantitative predictions about mountain structure or seismic behaviour fixed before the geological findings, rather than a metaphor whose meaning is adjusted to whichever feature of mountains a modern audience considers impressive.

Finding: the peg image and some real geological features can coexist. The direct words do not uniquely predict root depth, buoyancy, crustal support or modern seismic science.

21 / QURAN 55:19-20 AND 25:53: THE BARRIER IS NOT AN IMPERMEABLE WALL

Quran 55:19-20 and 25:53: the barrier is not an impermeable wall

Quran 55:19-20 describes two bodies of water which meet, yaltaqiyani, with a barzakh, barrier, between them and a statement that they do not transgress. Quran 25:53 explicitly distinguishes one water body as fresh and the other as salty, then uses barzakhan and a further expression of separation. The Arabic Corpus identifies the common barrier noun, but the two passages differ in detail and should not be treated as identical physical descriptions. [31] [32]

Real seawater and freshwater can form visible density gradients or a salt wedge at an estuary. Such boundaries do not create an absolutely impermeable division. NOAA explains that estuaries contain brackish water produced by mixing, and that salinity changes with tides and freshwater flow. This is an important independent physical fact: even in a stratified estuary, the fluids interact and exchange material. [33]

A strong reconciliation reads the Quran's barrier as an observable stratification or sustained distinction despite contact, rather than an assertion that no molecules can cross. That may be linguistically and observationally viable. The stronger miracle claim, however, usually requires the barrier to encode previously inaccessible oceanography. People living near estuaries could see or taste differences in salinity, and the verse contains no theory of density, turbulent mixing, haloclines or seasonal circulation.

Verdict: a poetic separation can be compatible with real salinity gradients. An impenetrable scientific boundary would contradict observed mixing, while the looser interpretation is too familiar and unspecific to prove supernatural foreknowledge.

22 / PRE-ISLAMIC CONCEPTS MUST BE CONSIDERED BEFORE ALLEGING INACCESSIBILITY

Pre-Islamic concepts must be considered before alleging inaccessibility

The key historical proposition in a scientific-miracle argument is not that a modern scientist can find a resemblance. It is that the original words clearly conveyed information unavailable through ordinary observation or earlier intellectual traditions. That assertion requires evidence about what people could reasonably know or infer before Islam. Genesis 1:6-10 contains an earlier literary separation of cosmic waters; Galen's extant work gives a pre-Islamic theory of staged fetal growth. Neither source proves direct Quranic borrowing, but each refutes a sweeping claim of complete pre-Islamic ignorance of the general motif. [20] [23] [24]

There is also a difference between a scientific discovery and a familiar observation. Water supports life, a fetus grows through visible changes and freshwater can contrast with salty water at a river mouth. None requires modern microscopy, satellites or molecular biology to be described in broad terms. The mere fact that something is true is not evidence that the speaker acquired it supernaturally; historical availability and linguistic specificity determine whether the example is surprising.

Arguments for literary dependence need more than a chronological parallel. A proven intermediary would require a plausible path of communication, evidence that the tradition was transmitted in a usable language or community, and details too distinctive to be accidental or independently observed. By the same standard, an advocate of supernatural foreknowledge must demonstrate that the surviving text commits to a nontrivial physical model that could not plausibly have arisen without revelation.

Historical verdict: earlier intellectual and scriptural contexts make broad scientific originality claims unpersuasive. The actual route of any particular motif into the Quran remains a separate research question rather than a fact inferred from similarity alone.

23 / FALSIFICATION CRITERIA, ALTERNATIVES AND SEPARATE VERDICTS

Falsification criteria, alternatives and separate verdicts

The strongest alternative is not that every verse gives a textbook formula. It is that an ancient text uses concise, flexible natural language to reveal true features of the world and that several converging correspondences together support divine origin. That position can be argued. Its evidential weakness is that each example must be independently constrained before a modern finding is chosen; adding more flexible matches does not remove the same basic ambiguity or ensure that the candidate examples were selected without bias. [19] [21] [23]

A defensible prospective test would identify a verse's original-language meaning, record what people understood before the relevant discovery, list physical alternatives that the text rules out, and specify the observation that would prove the reading wrong. A truly novel quantitative or otherwise discriminating prediction interpreted and published before empirical confirmation would require a major reassessment. A retrospective rendering which also accommodates failed alternatives does not have that evidential force.

Text-first verdict: these passages contain actual linguistic images that must be read in their immediate contexts, not modern technical paraphrases assumed in advance. Scientific verdict: observed cosmology, embryology, anatomy, geology and hydrology do not establish the claimed precise miracles; some literal readings are difficult to reconcile with contemporary evidence. Historical verdict: comparable general ideas existed before Islam, while specific literary dependence requires further evidence. Biblical theological verdict: no claimed scientific resemblance overrides the project's Biblical-Christian test of revelation, and none independently authenticates a competing prophetic claim.

Falsification: a documented pre-discovery, independently understandable Quranic prediction of an otherwise unknown physical fact which excludes realistic alternatives would change the positive miracle assessment. Conversely, a well-evidenced literal contradiction of an unambiguous Quranic physical proposition would strengthen a specific science-error assessment. Ambiguity should not be treated as a proof in either direction. [34]

25 / CONCLUSION

The evidence does not establish extraordinary scientific foreknowledge

Text-first verdict: The passages considered here contain broad cosmological, developmental and anatomical descriptions, but they do not uniquely encode modern scientific discoveries at the level required to establish inaccessible foreknowledge. [1] [3] [9] [13]

This does not assert that every poetic expression is scientifically false or that supernatural revelation is metaphysically impossible. It rejects the leap from a flexible ancient phrase to retrospective proof of divine origin. Separate investigations examine the supernatural purpose of celestial projectiles and extraordinary historical miracle claims. Those claims require independent corroboration rather than scientific facts borrowed from unrelated modern discoveries. [16] [17] [18]
