RESEARCH / CI-006

When inheritance shares exceed the estate.

A worked case shows why the raw fractions in Quran 4:11-12 cannot always be distributed without an additional rule.

01 / THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Can every stated fraction be paid in full?

VERIFIED ARITHMETICAL RESULT

In a recognised inheritance case, the shares named in Quran 4:11-12 total 27/24, or 112.5% of the estate. Those original fractions cannot all be paid in full from one estate. Islamic jurists use a proportional reduction called awl to resolve such cases. The Quranic verses themselves do not explicitly set out that reduction.

Consider a man who dies leaving a wife, his father, his mother and three daughters, with no sons. Assume that debts and any permissible bequests have already been dealt with and that these are the relevant heirs. The rule for three daughters avoids the separate translation dispute over whether the phrase in 4:11 includes exactly two daughters. [1] [2]

02 / SCRIPTURAL ALLOCATION

What fractions do the verses assign?

Quran 4:11 assigns two-thirds of the estate collectively to three daughters. It separately assigns one-sixth to each parent when the deceased has children. Quran 4:12 assigns one-eighth to a widow when her deceased husband has children. [1] [2]

The total is not an allegation about a mistranslated word. It is the direct sum of the displayed fractions: 16 + 4 + 4 + 3 = 27 parts when the estate contains 24 parts. In a hypothetical estate worth $24,000, the nominal entitlements sum to $27,000.

03 / THE JURISTIC SOLUTION

How does Islamic law make the shares fit?

Sunni jurists have long applied awl, which proportionately reduces the prescribed shares if their total exceeds the distributable estate. A written Islamic legal explanation applies the method to precisely this family configuration. [3] The general rule is also described in legal responses to the apparent excess. [4]

Rather than award 16/24, 4/24, 4/24 and 3/24 of the estate, the method distributes 16/27, 4/27, 4/27 and 3/27. The new fractions sum to one and can all be paid.

This solution is mathematically coherent. It also changes each heir's effective fraction from the face-value fraction in the verses. Other inheritance traditions may handle priority and reduction differently; this article addresses the widely documented Sunni solution.

04 / TEXT VERSUS JURISPRUDENCE

Does the Quran explicitly provide the reduction formula?

The cited verses specify shares and make distribution subject to debts and bequests. They do not state an explicit instruction such as: when the listed shares exceed the whole estate, reduce every listed entitlement in proportion to the excess. [1] [2]

That omission does not mean Muslims lack a working inheritance system. It means the practical system relies on juristic interpretation beyond the fractions stated in these two verses. Describing that distinction as a mathematical failure in the operational law would be inaccurate; describing the textual fractions as universally distributable without adjustment would also be inaccurate.

What is actually established

A later interpretive rule resolves the over-allocation. Whether law is expected to require post-scriptural elaboration is a theological and jurisprudential question, not a calculation that can settle itself.

In the worked example, the nominal shares are 16/24 for the three daughters, 4/24 for each parent, and 3/24 for the widow. They total 27/24, so even if the estate were free from debts and other complications, not all entitlements can be paid in the stated proportions. The awl remedy makes the denominator 27, reducing every claimant's effective fraction. [1] [2] [3]

This matters for evaluating the claim of an exhaustive, directly usable divine allocation formula: the original fractions and the enforceable fractions are not identical in this situation. An Islamic jurisprudential response that law may legitimately be completed through interpretation is coherent, but it must acknowledge that additional interpretive work is necessary. The criticism is not that jurists cannot distribute an estate; it is that these verses alone do not provide a self-contained solution to every permitted set of heirs.

05 / A SECOND INHERITANCE CONFIGURATION CONFIRMS THE OVER-ALLOCATION

A second inheritance configuration confirms the over-allocation

The problem is not confined to one artificially arranged family. Consider a deceased woman who leaves a husband, mother and two full sisters, with no children or father and with the applicable inheritance conditions satisfied. Quran 4:12 gives a childless widower one-half; Quran 4:11 gives the mother one-sixth where siblings are present; Quran 4:176 assigns two sisters two-thirds in a kalalah case. The face-value shares total 1/2 + 1/6 + 2/3 = 4/3 of the estate. [5] [1] [2]

The legal definitions of kalalah, sibling classes and exclusion rules must be established before applying the figures; a simple list of relatives is not always sufficient. Under the stated assumptions, however, this is another instance in which paying the unadjusted fractions would require more money than exists. The fact that a juristic procedure can resolve the case is relevant, but it does not erase the excess in the initial face-value allocation.

06 / THE HISTORICAL DISAGREEMENT ABOUT AWL MATTERS

The historical disagreement about awl matters

An Islamic legal response preserves a significant early disagreement: an account attributes proportional awl to Umar, whereas Ibn Abbas is reported to have favoured an ordering in which some specified shares take priority over others rather than all being proportionately reduced. The legal explanation at Islam Question and Answer sets out this disagreement while defending the predominant Sunni method. This is evidence that interpreters recognised a real allocation problem and debated its legal remedy. [4]

The difference is substantive. With a proportional remedy, all affected face-value fractions decline. With a priority remedy, some are protected and the remainder is reduced more heavily or excluded. Neither mechanism is the literal instruction "pay each listed percentage in full" when the percentages exceed 100%. Historical disagreement over the remedy therefore strengthens the narrow criticism that these verses alone do not give a unique complete executable algorithm.

It would be inaccurate to infer from disagreement that all Islamic inheritance practice is arbitrary. Legal systems routinely use interpretive principles. The claim under scrutiny is the stronger assertion that the relevant Quranic sentences already specify every required allocation step without recourse to interpretation.

07 / UNALLOCATED RESIDUE IS A SEPARATE PROBLEM FROM OVER-ALLOCATION

Unallocated residue is a separate problem from over-allocation

The inverse situation also matters. A deceased person may leave fixed-share heirs whose stated fractions sum to less than the estate. Islamic jurisprudence has rules for residual heirs and for return of a remainder, known as radd, when relevant; these are different problems from awl. The existence of a remainder does not produce the same mathematical impossibility as an excess, and no general critique should confuse them. Quran 4:11-12 and 4:176 specify a range of fixed shares and classes, not a self-contained algorithm for all possible family structures. [5]

The hidden dependency is legal: before anyone can run the fractions, they must identify qualified heirs, exclusions, debts, applicable bequests, residual priority and which jurisprudential school governs the distribution. A statement that a fraction is divinely assigned is not sufficient to calculate the full estate unless those surrounding conditions have already been settled.

The test should therefore be framed as a documentation issue, not the claim that Muslim jurists cannot perform elementary arithmetic. The mathematics of the demonstrated shortfall is indisputable; its implications for the completeness of revelation depend on what the Quran promises to give its readers.

08 / WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A GENUINE DEFENCE OF COMPLETENESS?

What would count as a genuine defence of completeness?

A successful argument that the Quran is an exhaustive inheritance allocation code would have to identify a Quranic instruction that selects the priority or proportionality rule for every permitted excess, prove that this principle follows from the wording rather than merely from later practice, and account for historical disagreements over the remedy. An argument that the Quran intentionally supplies general duties while legal experts legitimately develop implementing rules is different and more plausible; it concedes that the verses alone are incomplete as a numerical programme.

This distinction matters to Quranic claims that guidance is explained in detail. Quran 16:89 describes the Book as an explanation of all things in a broader religious context. It is not sound to pretend this is an explicit promise to include every legal formula. Nevertheless, a defender asserting that no extra interpretive machinery is necessary for these specific shares faces an unambiguous practical counterexample. [6]

Evidence assessment: the numerical excess is verified from the primary text; awl is a real juristic correction; the question of the Quran's claimed comprehensiveness is a theological and interpretive dispute, not a reason to deny the fractions.

05 / CONCLUSION

A real over-allocation, and a real legal remedy

The stated fractions in Quran 4:11-12 exceed 100% for this family configuration. The figures cannot simultaneously operate as unreduced percentages of the same estate. The traditional awl procedure corrects the allocation by proportionally reducing the shares.

The defensible criticism is therefore precise: the Quranic fractions are not a self-sufficient executable allocation rule for every case. A claim that the verses alone always prescribe distributable, unadjusted percentages is false. The stronger claim that Islamic law cannot calculate or distribute such an estate is also false.

Verdict: The Quranic fractions fail as a complete unadjusted allocation rule. In the demonstrated valid family configuration, the prescribed shares total 112.5 percent. Sunni jurisprudence resolves the problem through awl, but that is an additional allocation rule rather than a direct instruction in 4:11-12.
