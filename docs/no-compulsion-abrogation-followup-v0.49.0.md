# CI-017 - Quranic abrogation and the proposed supersession of 2:256
Evidence addendum for release v0.49.0, 8 November 2026.

## Source-first correction
The Quran explicitly recognises replacement of revelation: [2:106](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=2&verse=106) uses *nansakh*, while [16:101](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=16&verse=101) describes one *ayah* being substituted for another. The Quranic basis for a form of *naskh* is not an external invention. The historical details of technical legal abrogation remain interpretive.

## The actual proposed cancellation
The late Medinan Surah 9 includes [9:29](https://quran.com/9/29), which directs fighting specified People of the Book until jizya is paid while subdued. A later coercive command can constrain the reach of a broader non-compulsion statement without necessarily requiring a formal repeal of a protection against forced inward conversion.

The [commentary attributed to al-Tabari on 2:256](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura2-aya256.html) records reports that it was abrogated after fighting People of the Book was ordered in Surah Bara'ah. Al-Tabari also records an interpretation protecting those from whom jizya is accepted and explicitly prefers the narrower operative reading. [Ibn al-Jawzi's later summary](https://quran.com/en/2%3A256/answers/is-no-compulsion-in-religion-abrogated) distinguishes operative, qualified and abrogated views; its editorial "verse of the sword" commonly refers to 9:5.

[Yohanan Friedmann](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/tolerance-and-coercion-in-islam/is-there-no-compulsion-in-religion/EA427167521C238E7A746E9F7669) documents traditions naming 9:29 as abrogator of the earlier rule for People of the Book, while 9:5 or 9:73 occur in competing formulations. [Abu al-Qasim al-Khoei](https://al-islam.org/al-bayan-fi-tafsir-al-quran-prolegomena-quran-sayyid-abu-al-qasim-al-khoei/10-abrogation-quran) records the 9:29 thesis and contests technical cancellation. These are evidence of actual disagreements about application, not independent eyewitness verification of revelation dates or a Quranic clause naming its own replacement.

## Discriminating tests
1. Is the protection in 2:256 directed against coerced confession, or all political restrictions motivated by religious identity?
2. Does 9:29 legally contradict that precise protection, or impose a different political obligation alongside it?
3. Is the proposed chronological ordering established with evidence stronger than a retrospective literary sequence?
4. Which particular verse is supposed to supersede 2:256: 9:29, 9:5, or 9:73? The alternatives cannot be treated as interchangeable.

## Verdict
**Text-first:** the Quran affirms replacement of revelation and includes a later coercive jizya command. **Historically attested interpretation:** some exegetes explicitly held that the latter fighting provisions abrogated 2:256; others maintained a restricted operative rule. **Legal and logical verdict:** the broad claim of unconditional modern religious equality fails under either interpretation; the specific technical abrogation of 2:256 by 9:29 is a contested inference, not an explicit Quranic declaration.
