# CI-046 - Joseph, women cutting their hands and Jewish narrative development

Primary-language and chronology audit for v0.57.0, 8 November 2026.

## Wording in the Quran

[Quran 12:31](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=12&verse=31) directly states that a knife was given to every woman, that they cut their hands and acclaimed Joseph as a noble angel. It does **not** name oranges, citrons, a peeling action or the woman's name Zulaykha. [Quran 12:50-51](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=12&verse=50) connects the women with plotting and attempted seduction but does not expressly identify the injuries as deliberate suicide threats.

## Earlier Biblical and Jewish witnesses

- [Genesis 39:6-20](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.39.6-20) records beauty, temptation, resistance and accusation but not the women's banquet.
- [Genesis Rabbah 98:18](https://www.sefaria.org/Bereshit_Rabbah.98.18) reports Egyptian women wanting to see Joseph, providing an earlier literary motif. It does not contain the hand injury.
- [Tanhuma Vayeshev 5:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Midrash_Tanchuma%2C_Vayeshev.5.2) reports Egyptian women, citrons, knives and cut hands. This is the original Hebrew literary witness, not a contemporary Egyptian document.
- [My Jewish Learning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/midrash-tanhuma/) and [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_12632.html) distinguish early Palestinian traditions from later Tanhuma recensions, including editorial dating around the ninth century. Do not describe the printed text as a physically dated fifth-century manuscript.

## Aramaic poetry and scholarly dating

- [Meir Bar-Ilan (2016)](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/s%C5%ABrat-y%C5%ABsuf-xii-and-some-of-its-possible-jewish-sources-2/) proposes a fourth-to-sixth-century Byzantine-era composition for developed Aramaic acrostic poems including women's banquet and injuries. The linguistic dating is relevant but not a physically carbon-dated copy containing the precise stanza.
- [James Kugel (1990)](https://www.jameskugel.com/books/in-potiphars-house/) distinguishes variants and is more cautious about the original age of the injury scene.
- [Charbeel Rizk (2021), pp. 129-131](https://digital.ub.uni-paderborn.de/download/pdf/7189557.pdf) discusses this divergence. The primary manuscript of the poem was not independently collated in this audit.
- [Mustansir Mir (2021)](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/12/8/619) evaluates the deliberate injury interpretation based on Quranic context; intentionality is not expressly fixed in the Arabic.
- [Edwin Goldberg (2024)](https://thetorah.com/article/egyptian-women-captivated-by-josephs-beauty-cut-their-hands-slicing-citrons) distinguishes Genesis Rabbah, Tanhuma and the much later Sefer HaYashar. [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://jewishencyclopedia.herokuapp.com/articles/8803-joseph) notes signs of Arabic influence in the late version.

## Conclusions

**Text-first:** Knives and cut hands are explicit in Quran 12:31. Citrus fruit, a named Zulaykha and an exact intention for the injuries are not.

**Historical:** An earlier Jewish Egyptian-women motif is clear. The fully developed scene is exceptionally close in Aramaic poems and Tanhuma, but the exact pre-Islamic physical witness is not authenticated in these cited materials. Bar-Ilan argues for early composition; Kugel is more cautious. A particular copying route and an independent Egyptian witness are not demonstrated.

**Biblical:** Genesis does not report the reception, although its silence is not an express historical denial. A later story is not independently authenticated Biblical history by being repeated in another scripture.

**Falsification criteria:** A physically dated early witness to the full Aramaic injury text could strengthen priority; a later demonstrable first composition could weaken it; a contemporary Egyptian document would change the historical assessment.
