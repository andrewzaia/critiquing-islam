# CI-050 - Quran 18:86 source and claim audit

## Source layers

- Quran 18:83-102: primary narrative of Dhul-Qarnayn's westward and eastward journeys, then the barrier.
- Quran 18:86: the received Arabic reads that he reached the Sun's setting-place and found it setting in a spring. The observer-based interpretation is plausible but the Arabic does not contain an explicit word 'as if'.
- Quranic Arabic Corpus: identifies the verbs `balagha`, `wajadahā`, `taghrubu` and the noun `ʿayn` with their grammatical functions.
- Qira'at: `ḥamiʾatin` is muddy; `ḥāmiyatin` is hot. The variation concerns the spring's quality and does not remove the setting-place scene.
- Al-Tabari: later Arabic commentary attests awareness of the competing readings, not independent proof of the journey's historicity.
- Quran 18:90: a second eastern journey reaches the Sun's rising-place, making a single-verse explanation incomplete.

## Historical comparison

Kevin van Bladel argues that Quran 18:83-102 reworks the Syriac Alexander Legend, including a western water boundary, eastern journey and northern barrier. The Syriac narrative and the Quran differ in detail. The proposed Syriac composition date around 629-630 and the traditional chronology of Surah 18 generate a genuine chronological question. Tommaso Tesei and other scholarship examine political and literary contexts. Shared late antique narrative tradition remains a serious alternative to a proven direct textual intermediary.

## Claim matrix

| Proposition | Evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arabic explicitly says 'as if' | No corresponding Arabic phrase in 18:86 | Not supported |
| A muddy spring is in the Hafs reading | Quran 18:86 and morphology | Verified |
| A hot spring is also transmitted | Canonical qira'at comparison | Verified |
| The Sun physically enters a spring | Requires literalisation of observer-centred narrative | Not established |
| Quran 18:86 predicts modern astronomy | No novel measurement or predictive mechanism | Not established |
| Narrative relates to Alexander tradition | Cumulative three-stage itinerary and specialist research | Likely |
| Direct copying of a specific Syriac document is proven | Composition chronology and transmission pathway disputed | Not demonstrated |

## Falsification and further work

A dated early Arabic example of the same construction in an unambiguously optical scene would strengthen the observational reading. A securely dated early literal interpretation would strengthen a historical-cosmology reading. A dated intermediary or stronger verbal correspondence would help distinguish direct literary dependence from a common story tradition. No manuscript collation of the exact 18:86 reading was performed for this milestone; do not imply otherwise.
