# CI-005 - Torah, Gospel and pre-Islamic textual witnesses

Evidence audit for v0.38.0, 7 November 2026.

## Scope and burden

This investigation tests the conjunction of two sets of Quranic claims: (1) the Torah and Gospel are divine revelations whose communities possess, read, consult and should apply them; (2) some people distort, misattribute, conceal or forget material. The question is not whether all Biblical manuscripts are identical or whether Christian revelation is true by definition. It is whether a wholesale corruption theory can explain both the Quran's present-tense appeals and the independently surviving texts.

A historical charge of corruption needs a named reading, a proposed original, a date, a mechanism and witnesses. A later text's disagreement with an earlier text does not by itself prove the earlier copy was changed.

## Material witnesses and precise scope

- **4Q11 (4QpaleoGen-Exod):** Qumran Paleo-Hebrew Genesis/Exodus witness dated by handwriting to the second or first century BCE; Hila Dayfani, *Textus* 30 (2021), DOI 10.1163/2589255X-BJA10017. This source also documents minor exegetical readings, so the existence of an ancient Torah tradition does not imply a single invariant wording.
- **4Q22 (4QpaleoExodm):** pre-Samaritan Exodus scroll with harmonising expansions, described by Lawrence Schiffman. This is evidence of real textual diversity long before Islam, not evidence of a later global replacement.
- **Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaa):** pre-Christian Hebrew prophetic manuscript, approximately 125 BCE, digitised by the Israel Museum. Isaiah belongs to the Prophets, not the five-book Torah; it independently shows older Jewish Biblical transmission.
- **Codex Vaticanus (GA 03):** fourth-century Greek Bible, with surviving Gospel material and some missing or later-supplied parts. Catalogued at CSNTM and the Vatican Library.
- **Codex Sinaiticus (GA 01):** fourth-century Greek Bible, with an intact New Testament and corrections. The official Codex Sinaiticus Project transcribes John 20:28 on the physical witness.
- **Codex Alexandrinus (GA 02):** fifth-century Greek Biblical codex catalogued by the British Library.
- **Papyrus 66 (P. Bodmer II):** late second or third-century extensive manuscript of John, catalogued by CSNTM. It does not establish a precise composition year for John.
- **Papyrus 52 (P. Rylands 457):** much smaller early John 18 fragment. It should not be invoked to claim direct preservation of the entire Johannine Gospel or of John 1 or John 20.
- **Septuagint:** ancient Jewish Greek translation tradition with variations compared with Hebrew manuscripts, discussed in the *Oxford Handbook of the Septuagint* (2021).

Manuscripts document transmitted wording in their copying period. They are not independent eyewitness certificates of narrated events or guarantees of the theological truth of their claims.

## Actual variation and an improperly broad corruption inference

The longer Trinitarian clause at 1 John 5:7-8 is a genuine history-of-text problem, cited via Galiza and Reeve (2018); the longer ending of Mark and John 7:53-8:11 are other commonly studied textual variants. These cases demand passage-specific witness comparison. They demonstrate that variants exist, not that the divine language in John 1:1 or Thomas's confession in John 20:28 was first invented after Islam.

Likewise, the existence of a New Testament book before Islam does not prove it was originally composed in exactly the surviving wording. An alteration before the earliest physical witness is possible; to sustain that hypothesis requires positive earlier witness evidence or a specifically argued historical mechanism.

## Ranked models under the balance of probabilities

1. **Partial integrity, interpretive conflict and identifiable isolated variation:** consistent with the Quran's contemporaneous appeals and with real ancient witnesses. This model has positive documentary support for old text and documented variants.
2. **Quranic overriding theological authority:** internally coherent as a religious claim, but circular when offered as independent evidence that an earlier manuscript reading was forged.
3. **Complete loss of the original Gospel prior to the Quran:** requires a demonstrated alternative original or a satisfactory explanation of 5:47's actionable instruction. The mere possibility of a lost record supplies neither.
4. **Universal coordinated post-Islamic replacement:** contradicted for substantial Biblical material by surviving pre-Islamic manuscripts. Claims of earlier wholesale replacement require separate pre-manuscript evidence and a traceable timeline.

## Verdicts

**Text-first:** The Quran presents Torah and Gospel material as usable by religious audiences while also condemning concrete acts of misrepresentation and forging. The latter texts cannot automatically be widened into an assertion that every earlier written copy had been replaced.

**Historical:** Hebrew Torah witnesses and Greek Christian Gospel manuscripts predate Islam by centuries. They exhibit textual variation but do not provide evidence for an undifferentiated universal corruption claim. Specific post-Islamic origin claims about already attested writings fail chronology.

**Theological:** The Quran may claim authority to judge earlier revelation. That is not independently establishing which earlier written sentence was falsely inserted. The burden remains passage-by-passage and should be applied equally to Islamic and Christian source history.
