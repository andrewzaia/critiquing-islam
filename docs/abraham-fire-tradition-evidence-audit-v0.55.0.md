# CI-044 - Abraham, the fire and earlier Jewish narrative

Primary-text, manuscript and Biblical source audit for v0.55.0, 8 November 2026.

## Original Quranic claim

Quran 21:51-70 expressly narrates Abraham's demolition of idols, his rhetorical challenge about the large idol, the proposal to burn him (21:68), and the divine command to fire, *ya naru kuni bardan wa-salaman ala Ibrahima* (21:69). [Quranic Arabic Corpus, 21:69](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=21&verse=69). Quran 29:24 expressly claims God saved Abraham from fire; 37:97 adds a constructed *bunyan*, which does not lexically itself mean a furnace. The Quranic fire narrative does not name Nimrod. Verse 2:258 has an unnamed opponent; making him Nimrod is a separate inference and should never be attributed to the Quran's Arabic.

## Earliest Biblical and literary layers

- [Genesis 11:26-32](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.11.26-32) mentions Haran dying in Ur of the Chaldeans; it does not report a fire ordeal involving Abraham.
- [Joshua 24:2-3](https://www.sefaria.org/Joshua.24.2-3) supplies ancestral idolatry, not an idol-shop episode.
- [Jubilees 12:1-8](https://www.sefaria.org/Book_of_Jubilees.12.1-8) develops an anti-idol conversation with Terah; [12:12-14](https://www.sefaria.org/Book_of_Jubilees.12.12-14) has **Abraham burning the idol house and Haran dying** attempting to save the objects. Abraham is not the victim saved from flames in this episode. [IAA 4Q216](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q216-1?locale=en) independently establishes an early Jubilees manuscript, not the exact text of every verse in chapter 12.
- [Biblical Antiquities 6:16-18](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-biblical-antiquities-of-philo/chapter-vi) recounts **Abraham surviving a furnace** in a Tower of Babel setting. Jectan/Joktan is the named antagonist, with a divine earthquake and surrounding casualties. [University of Pennsylvania overview](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/courses/525/LAB.htm) and [Cambridge source analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/outside-the-old-testament/pseudophilo-liber-antiquitatum-biblicarum/4FF8D80025BCF8D61342A4FE62B467A6) distinguish a likely first- or second-century composition from surviving eleventh-to-fifteenth-century Latin witnesses.
- [Genesis Rabbah 38:13](https://www.sefaria.org/Bereshit_Rabbah.38.13) includes the large-idol rhetorical device, Terah handing Abraham to Nimrod, the furnace, Abraham's survival and Haran's death. The collection is generally dated to the fifth century, but individual tradition dates can differ. [Mohr Siebeck](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/genesis-rabbah-in-text-and-context-9783161547034/).
- [Jerome on Genesis 11:28](https://historicalchristian.faith/by_father.php?file=Jerome%2FHebrew%2520Questions%2520on%2520Genesis.html) records the Jewish fire tradition before Islam. [Daniel 3:19-27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Daniel%203%3A19-27&version=KJV) is an earlier canonical furnace-rescue motif involving **other people**; possible literary influence is not a proven direct source.

## Historical chronology and precision

| Witness | Literary date or description | What it actually establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genesis / Joshua | Canonical Biblical tradition | Ur, Haran, Terah and former idol worship; no narrated Abrahamic furnace |
| Jubilees | Pre-Islamic Jewish composition; some Hasmonean manuscript witnesses | Earlier Abraham-idol material and Haran's death in fire; the scroll does not prove each line of 12:12-14 |
| Pseudo-Philo, Biblical Antiquities | Generally placed in first or second century AD; Latin manuscripts much later | Earlier written narrative of Abraham miraculously unharmed in furnace; no independent eyewitness |
| Jerome | Late fourth / early fifth century AD | Christian record of circulating Jewish interpretation |
| Genesis Rabbah | Commonly fifth-century Palestinian redaction | Expanded Nimrod, idol-shop and furnace story |
| Quran | Seventh-century textual tradition | Explicit fire rescue claim, without naming Nimrod in these verses |

## Evidence and limits

**[Verified textual observation]** Quran 21:69 directly states the divine command to fire and 29:24 directly reports rescue. The midrashic Nimrod name is absent from the Quran's fire account.

**[Verified literary priority]** Pseudo-Philo and Genesis Rabbah preserve pre-Islamic material relevant to the furnace rescue, with Jubilees showing a distinct earlier fire episode. The large-idol rhetorical exchange is a particularly specific narrative correspondence between Quran 21 and Genesis Rabbah 38.

**[Likely historical explanation]** Transmission and adaptation within circulating Jewish and late-antique narrative material parsimoniously accounts for the features without invoking fresh supernatural disclosure. An exact Hebrew-to-Arabic copy path and the full set of intermediaries have not been established.

**[Not established]** An independent contemporary witness to Abraham surviving a furnace, a particular manuscript accessed by Muhammad, intentional fabrication, or demonstrable authorship of every component of the narrated episode.

**Biblical theological assessment:** Genesis, Joshua, Acts and Daniel do not positively establish Abraham's specific furnace miracle. The earlier Jewish narratives are valuable witnesses to history of interpretation but have no independent Biblical doctrinal authority as accounts of Abraham's deeds.

**Falsification / revision conditions:** Independently dated evidence connecting an ancient physical ordeal to Abraham, or a firmly dated intermediary with the precise distinctive Quranic variant, would require revisiting the historical or transmission assessments respectively. Mere repetition in later commentary would not.

Source quotations and paraphrases are deliberately limited; manuscript witnesses and reconstructed composition dates are never conflated.
