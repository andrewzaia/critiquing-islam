# CI-012 - Quran 65:4, waiting periods and marriage age
Source-critical research audit, v0.50.0, 8 November 2026.

## Precise question
Quran 65:4 sets a waiting period for a group that has not menstruated without specifying a minimum age. Quran 33:49 exempts divorce before marital contact from a waiting period. These are primary-text propositions. Whether a specific early marriage occurred, whether early scholars permitted it and whether the rule meets modern child-protection standards are distinct questions.

## Original language and local context
- [Quran 65:4, word by word](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=65&verse=4): *wa-allai lam yahidna* uses a feminine relative pronoun, negative *lam* and a feminine plural jussive imperfect from the root ḥ-y-ḍ. The clause does not contain a word for adult, child or a numerical age.
- [Quran 33:49, word by word](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=33&verse=49): *min qabli an tamassuhunna*, before touching, is the Quranic expression in a marriage-and-divorce context. The exemption for this pre-contact divorce makes the waiting-period class in 65:4 a significant issue, while not proving the facts of any named marriage.
- [Quran 2:236](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=2&verse=236) and [2:237](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=2&verse=237) regulate pre-contact divorce with different financial conditions. [Quran 65:1](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=65&verse=1) requires counting waiting periods, and [2:228](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=2&verse=228) gives a cycle-based period. A legal contract is not identical to consummation.
- [Quran 4:6](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=4&verse=6) includes reaching marriageable age and *rushd*, responsible judgement, but its immediate legal instruction concerns releasing an orphan's property. [Quran 24:58-59](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=24&verse=59) uses an explicit puberty distinction in household-privacy rules.

## Alternatives required for an evidence-based conclusion
[ACOG, Amenorrhea](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/amenorrhea-absence-of-periods) documents genuine medical conditions in which menstruation has not begun or has ceased. This shows that an exclusively prepubescent reading of every possible case would overstate the Arabic. It does not establish an explicit adults-only limit, and the Quran does not use ACOG's modern diagnostic terminology.

[Al-Tabari's Arabic tafsir](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura65-aya4.html) records the younger-girl category alongside varying explanations of *in irtabtum*, and explains the age category in relation to divorce following marital contact. [Al-Qurtubi's Arabic commentary](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/qortobi/sura65-aya4.html) also treats *al-saghira* and distinguishes conditions of uncertain bleeding. These commentaries provide historically dated interpretation, not eyewitness confirmation of seventh-century marriages or an unalterable authority for the original verse.

## Contractual law and evidence of practice
[Kecia Ali, Consent and Forced Marriage](https://www.brandeis.edu/projects/fse/muslim/consent.html), and her [survey of classical marriage contracts](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv3142td0.5), document classical juristic doctrines concerning guardianship, contractual authority and differences between schools. [Wael B. Hallaq](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/sharia/contracts-and-other-obligations/F3677D906C2D48F276FDA8168C82FF6F) distinguishes legal competence from a guardian's authority to contract. [Judith Tucker's study of court records in Nablus](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/036319908801300201) documents some specific practices in 1720-1856, but cannot be projected directly to the lifetime of Muhammad.

## Biblical and ethical criteria
[UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/protection/child-marriage) defines child marriage as marriage or a union involving a person under eighteen, and documents harms to education, health, welfare and agency. This present standard is not the Quran's unstated numerical rule. [Mark 10:13-16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2010%3A13-16&version=KJV) and [Matthew 18:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2018%3A6&version=KJV) give the website's explicitly Biblical moral comparison without pretending that they set a specific statutory age.

## Evidence classification
**Text-first:** the original 65:4 clause gives a post-divorce waiting period without restricting the non-menstruating category to adults.
**Legal inference:** 33:49 makes it credible that the waiting-period category can refer to post-contact divorces, including the younger cases interpreted in later scholarship.
**Historical:** recognised juristic interpretations and later court records are not independent evidence of the circumstances of a particular seventh-century marriage.
**Ethical:** contemporary child-protection and capacity-for-consent principles offer a clear additional standard against which historic legal accommodations can be criticised.

## Specific evidence that would change the verdict
A demonstrable original-language restriction excluding all minors from the 65:4 category, or a Quranic cross-reference establishing a universal binding age condition controlling its post-contact waiting-period rules, would materially affect the legal judgement. A specific historical allegation would need dated contemporary witnesses or reconstructable transmission, not this verse alone. Structural automation verifies references and navigation, not the underlying supernatural truth or exhaustive historical accuracy.
