# Noncanonical manuscript readings - v0.70.0

## Source access and scope

- [Rami Hussein Halaseh (2024), The Topkapı Qurʾān Manuscript H.S. 32](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783111467320), chapter 5, sections 5.3.7-5.3.8, Tables 5.4-5.5, pp. 133-141. The publisher description and [reproduced chapter text](https://dokumen.pub/the-topkap-qurn-manuscript-hs-32-history-text-and-variants-islam-thought-culture-and-society-7-1nbsped-3111455254-9783111455259.html) were consulted. The author counts at least 28 noncanonical readings with literary parallels and 12 not found in the major reading literature he surveyed. Those are his reported counts, not an independent census. The terms refer to the particular reading at the particular verse.
- [Éléonore Cellard, The Written Transmission of the Quran during Umayyad Times](https://www.academia.edu/62972357/The_Written_Transmission_of_the_Quran_during_Umayyad_Times_Contextualising_the_Codex_Amrensis_1), chapter 21, Tables 21.5 and 21.10, pp. 447 and 457. Accessible reproduced chapter text consulted. The two records here are selected from the shared pointing examples, not an independent count of all its unknown variants. Their verse locations fall within the Marcel 9 portion according to Table 21.3; exact folios have not been verified.

Neither original manuscript nor full facsimile was independently collated. Arabic snippets are normalised from the reported forms. Halaseh's and Cellard's categories are attributed scholarly findings, not a fresh exhaustive collation of every canonical and noncanonical reading source. Literary attributions are not independent contemporary proof of prophetic authorisation.

Other reported manuscripts offer related pointing or vocalisation evidence but are not added as separate catalogue records without detailed collation. ## Evidence verdict

Canonical acceptance does not account for all physical variation. Coherent repeated or shared readings challenge universal explanations by isolated error, without excluding error or shared ancestry in particular cases. A physical attestation establishes a written or marked form, not its prophetic authorisation. Proving intentional suppression requires independent evidence about a decision, its date and the reading affected. No specific new Samarkand reading is published without an accessible source locating it.

Next priorities: direct folio comparison, clearer manuscript evidence for uncertain entries and consolidation of the growing catalogue by transmission, physical state and semantic effect.
