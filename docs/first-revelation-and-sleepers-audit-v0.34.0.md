# Evidence audit - CI-038 and CI-039 (v0.34.0)

## CI-038: first revelation and messenger identity

Quran 96:1-5 contains the recitation command, not the full cave narrative. Quran 2:97, 81:19-25, 26:192-195 and 26:210-212 identify a trustworthy messenger and deny a devilish source. These are internal claims. The report attributed to Aisha in Sahih al-Bukhari 3 describes Hira, repeated pressings, Muhammad's fear, Khadijah's reassurance and Waraqah's interpretation. Khadijah and Waraqah are not portrayed as observing the cave encounter. Parallel reports in later hadith compilations are not automatically independent eyewitness testimony.

The distinction between physical intervention and commissioning a message contrary to earlier scripture is substantive. The earlier Christian criterion in Galatians 1:8 explicitly warns about a contrary gospel brought even by an angel; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 identifies the earlier apostolic message. Quran 4:157 contains the contrasting crucifixion claim. This establishes a serious theological and textual test, but the Quranic denial must be analysed in context, and conflict does not prove a particular supernatural source. 2 Corinthians 11:14 supplies a model of a deceptive spiritual messenger, not independent evidence of Satan at Hira. Equal evidentiary burdens do not justify fabricated equal probabilities.

## CI-039: the Seven Sleepers and the Companions of the Cave

Quran 18:9-26 shares a distinctive sequence with an earlier Christian legend of persecuted youths, prolonged sleep, awakening and an errand for food. Specialist studies by Nicholas Sims-Williams (Encyclopaedia Iranica), Sidney Griffith (JIQSA 6, published 2022) and Marcel Poorthuis (2020) support pre-Islamic literary attestation. The Sleepers homily associated with Jacob of Serugh (died 521) belongs to this earlier tradition. Scholarly research identifies its later manuscript transmission in Vatican Syriac 115, folios 80r-83v; the manuscript was not directly collated for this release.

Earlier literary priority, likely narrative engagement, demonstrable direct copying of a particular surviving version, and the historicity of centuries-long sleep are different questions. The first is supported; the second is strongly supported; the third and fourth are not established by the cited material. Differences in the number of sleepers, the Quran's lack of an Ephesus identification and variation in emphasis must be reported rather than erased.

## Verification limits

The automated checks guard research depth, sequential citations, source use, navigation and integration in the site. They do not establish philological conclusions, firsthand manuscript inspection, a dated historical miracle or the source of a supernatural event. Online citations were consulted selectively, and not every external link has been individually revalidated. No changes were made to the Quran explorer, Qira'at corpus, Quran.Foundation integration, cloud secrets or deployment configuration.
