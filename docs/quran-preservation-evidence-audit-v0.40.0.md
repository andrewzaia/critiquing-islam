# CI-003 - Quranic preservation, manuscripts and canonical readings

Evidence audit for v0.40.0, 7 November 2026.

## Proposition and method

The historical question is not answered by accepting or rejecting a broad word such as preservation. The source claims must be distinguished: preservation of the main message, preservation of one written consonantal archetype, preservation of every historical manuscript's exact text, preservation of all alleged original utterances, preservation of multiple canonical vocalisations, and divine causation of the transmitted result.

The Quran and its Arabic wording are the starting point. Quran 15:9 claims that the Reminder is revealed and guarded by God. Quran 41:41-42 and 85:21-22 provide related theological claims. These verses do not identify the exact printed edition, all permitted oral readings or a comparison method for individual historical manuscript differences.

## Primary witnesses and limits

| Source | Positive result | What is not established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Birmingham Mingana Islamic Arabic 1572a | Two early leaves containing portions of surahs 18, 19 and 20; parchment radiocarbon interval 568-645 CE at 95.4 percent | Neither an ink date nor a complete 114-surah autograph |
| Marijn van Putten (2019) | Shared rare orthographic details across 14 early witnesses support a common written Uthmanic archetype | The complete range of original oral recitations |
| Hythem Sidky (2021) | Reconstructed regional manuscript families linked to Syria, Medina, Basra and Kufa | Perfect letter-for-letter regional identity or the truth of every later report |
| Sadeghi and Goudarzi (2012) | The Sanaa palimpsest's lower text is a distinct early non-Uthmanic textual type | Identity of a complete earlier original Quran or proof of deliberate suppression |
| Asma Hilali (2017) | A critical edition of the two Sanaa textual layers, with material and reconstruction limits | Certainty for illegible or disputed fragments |
| Van Putten (2022) | Canonical readers normally follow rasm, with rare departures and regional explanations | That any particular exceptional reading was spoken by Muhammad |
| Quran 2:184 reading comparison | Singular and plural forms for needy person / needy people | A necessarily contradictory fasting rule |
| Quran 10:22 reading comparison | Different verbal roots for travel and dispersal | An automatic contradiction or known deliberate manuscript alteration |
| Déroche (2009), Codex Parisino-petropolitanus | A substantial early manuscript with critical textual transcription | A prophet-era autograph or a guarantee of every canonical vowel |

The Birmingham date applies to the death of the animal from which the parchment was prepared. The institution's primary record does not date the ink through radiocarbon analysis. The item is not the Sanaa palimpsest.

A common text type with shared unusual spellings is positive evidence that standardisation was early. A distinct contemporaneous text type is positive evidence that full initial uniformity is not the same as stability within the dominant copied branch. Repeated manuscripts descended from one exemplar are related witnesses, not independent new eyewitnesses to every original revelation.

## Historical Uthmanic reports

Bukhari 4986 and 4987 narrate a collection under Abu Bakr and a standardisation under Uthman that involved making official copies and burning alternatives. These are internally attributable reports of the historical programme, not direct first-century administrative records. Their broad outline receives important material support from the reconstructed shared archetype and regional families. The report does not permit us to recover the exact contents of all copies said to have been destroyed.

The study on Uthman's selection of an official text remains a separate investigation and is not duplicated here.

## Reading variation

An early Arabic consonantal rasm cannot simply be equated with one modern fully vocalised reading. Some canonical variation affects pronunciation, morphology or word forms. Quran.com documents the difference between singular *miskin* and plural *masakin* in Quran 2:184, but explains that the practical feeding result is understood as equivalent. Quran 10:22 contrasts forms of *yusayyirukum* and *yanshurukum* from distinct verbal roots, without establishing a contradiction of religious doctrine.

The historical conclusion is that canonical reading traditions are not one exactly identical vocalised sequence. This does not establish that every such difference is corruption. A claim that all were divinely authorised must be examined under the separate historical burden of showing their provenance.

## Reports of omitted revelation

- Quran 2:106 refers to replacement or forgetting.
- Quran 87:6-7 refers to divine control over what is remembered.
- Quran 75:17-19 refers to divine collection and recitation.
- Sahih Muslim 1452a attributes to Aisha recitations about ten and five sucklings.
- Sahih Muslim 1050 attributes to Abu Musa a memory of lost or forgotten lengthy passages.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 6829 attributes to Umar concern about an alleged stoning verse absent from the written Book.

The latter three are transmitted narratives, not surviving physical manuscripts of the alleged missing verses. A legal or theological interpretation of abrogation does not itself demonstrate a particular event of divinely directed omission. Conversely, a transmitted recollection is not independent physical proof of unintended textual loss.

## Evidence classification and verdicts

**[Verified]** An early written Uthmanic archetype is strongly supported by shared orthographic evidence.

**[Verified]** A non-Uthmanic early text type survives in the lower Sanaa writing.

**[Verified]** Regional consonantal differences and canonical reading variants are documented.

**[Verified]** Birmingham radiocarbon testing dates a partial manuscript's parchment rather than the entire original Quran or its ink.

**[Likely]** Early Uthmanic standardisation accounts for much of the manuscript evidence; the broad narrative is corroborated better than its individual late-reported details.

**Not demonstrated:** That every original recitation was preserved without change, that each disputed omitted text was divinely abrogated, or that manuscript agreement alone proves supernatural intervention.

**Not demonstrated:** Wholesale fabrication of the Quran centuries after the first Islamic period. That claim is weakened by real early written evidence.

The historical verdict should therefore reject an absolute, variant-free preservation assertion while accepting the well-evidenced early written standardisation. Neither finding is an argument for disrespecting Muslims as people.

## Falsification and technical limitations

An earlier independent, securely dated witness to a different Quranic text could change the reconstruction of the text's early history. More early manuscripts showing shared uncommon spellings strengthen the archetype reconstruction. Demonstrable independent pre-canonical recitation witnesses could strengthen an individual reading's historical provenance; a reading merely fitting an ambiguous consonantal form is not sufficient.

The site checks verify section count, bibliography sequence, citation usage, unique source URLs, content markers and site navigation. They do not authenticate oral traditions, establish the occurrence of a supernatural event, recover lost texts or exhaustively collate every manuscript.
