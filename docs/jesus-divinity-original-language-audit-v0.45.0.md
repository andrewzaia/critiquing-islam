# CI-010 original-language and manuscript evidence audit - v0.45.0

## Claim and source separation
Canonical New Testament documents directly give Jesus divine titles and prerogatives. Quran 5:72 rejects identifying God with the Messiah. The textual conflict is verifiable; whether the Christian or Quranic theology is divinely true is a separate question.

## Original-language tests
- [Quran 5:72](https://corpus.quran.com/treebank.jsp?chapter=5&verse=72) condemns the Arabic identification of God and Messiah. [Quran 4:171](https://corpus.quran.com/treebank.jsp?chapter=4&verse=171) has the word and spirit titles while rejecting divine sonship. [Quran 5:75](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=5&verse=75) uses the dual form for eating food.
- [John 1:1, SBL Greek](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%201%3A1&version=SBLGNT) has a pre-verbal anarthrous predicate theos. Its absence of article does not mechanically mean English "a god". The full discourse includes creation through the Word and the Word becoming flesh.
- [John 20:28, Greek](https://openbible.com/text/john/20-28.htm) says Thomas spoke to Jesus and addressed him as Lord and God.
- [Philippians 2:6, Greek](https://openbible.com/text/philippians/2-6.htm) includes morphe theou and the interpretively disputed harpagmon. [1 Corinthians 8:6, Greek](https://www.openbible.com/text/1_corinthians/8-6.htm) distinguishes the Father and Jesus while linking Christ to creation.

## Manuscripts and early reception
H. A. G. Houghton, [The Text of the Gospel and Letters of John](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/29525014/Houghton_OHJS_preprint.pdf), *Oxford Handbook of Johannine Studies* (2018), pp. 5-22, identifies the relevant God and Son witnesses for John 1:18, including P66, P75, Sinaiticus and Vaticanus. The readings and palaeographic date ranges must be distinguished. Physical copying is not proof of every event in the text.

[Ignatius, Ephesians 18:2](https://greekdoc.github.io/early/i-ephesians.html) describes Jesus as God and conceived by Mary in the middle recension of a generally early-second-century letter. [Pliny, Letters 10.96](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/pliny-letters/10-96) reports Christian song to Christ quasi deo, based on statements by people under interrogation. Ignatius is Christian textual testimony; Pliny is a Roman outsider reporting others' practice, not an eyewitness to Jesus.

## Alternative explanations and verdicts
The claim of a fourth-century invention of all divine Christology is contradicted by first- and early-second-century sources. The claim that early Christians had already misunderstood Jesus needs a specific earlier account and transmission evidence; Quranic disagreement is not proof of historical corruption.

**Text-first verdict:** the New Testament and Quran express conflicting claims. **Historical verdict:** early divine Christology and devotion are demonstrable, whereas a universal replacement of prior non-divine texts is not established. **Biblical theological verdict:** under the site's Christian framework, the canonical Scriptures support the deity of Christ and conflict with the Quran's correction.

## Falsification and limits
An authenticated early manuscript with a contrary original and evidence establishing its replacement could change an individual reading verdict. It would not automatically prove every teaching of an alleged lost Gospel. Automated validation covers structural integrity, citations and navigation; it does not certify every manuscript reading or any supernatural event.
