## Directly evidenced textual facts

- The Quran names Haman six times and consistently places him with Pharaoh and Moses. Arabic concordance: https://corpus.quran.com/search.jsp?q=con%3Ahaman .
- Quran 28:38 and 40:36-37 attribute a high-building instruction to Pharaoh addressing Haman. Compare the Arabic and translations: https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=28&verse=38 .
- The earlier book of Esther places Haman in the court of Ahasuerus in Persia, rather than in Egypt: https://www.sefaria.org/Esther.1?lang=en and https://www.sefaria.org/Esther.3?lang=en .
- Genesis 11:1-9 combines fired bricks and a heavenward tower. Its differences from the Quranic scene must not be overlooked: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2011%3A1-9&version=KJV .
- Numbers 16 places Korah in the Moses narrative; Quran 29:39 and 40:23-24 group Qarun with Pharaoh and Haman.

## Scholarly context and scope

Adam J. Silverstein's 2018 Oxford chapter discusses the reception of Haman in Jewish, Christian and Quranic narrative traditions: https://academic.oup.com/book/10288/chapter-abstract/158003986 . The publicly available chapter abstract, not the full paywalled chapter, was examined. The encyclopaedia reference differentiates the narrative settings: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/haman/ .

## Evidence boundaries

- Verified textual discrepancy: the Quran and Esther place a Haman in two different court settings.
- Plausible historical inference: literary reception or reassignment may explain the later narrative, but a particular copying chain was not independently reconstructed.
- Unverified claim: an Egyptian court official with a demonstrably equivalent name, appropriate date and court role has not been established by the cited material. This is not proof that no such person could ever have existed.
- Alleged hieroglyphic corroboration requires a dated artefact, museum or excavation identifier, photograph, Egyptian transcription, scholarly transliteration and a documented historical link to a relevant royal court.
- The source-structure and automated checks do not validate archaeology, the accuracy of every external source, or supernatural questions.
