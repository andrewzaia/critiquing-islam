# CI-048 evidence audit - v0.58.0

Date: 2026-11-10

## Claims separated

1. Quran 53:19-23 in its received form rejects al-Lat, al-Uzza and Manat as deities with divine authority.
2. Quran 22:52 explicitly refers to Satan casting something into a messenger's tamanna or umniyya and to God's removal of it.
3. Al-Tabari transmits attributed accounts that Muhammad spoke a formula praising the deities' intercession during an earlier recitation.
4. The actual occurrence of that early recitation is a different historical claim, lacking an independently dated eyewitness record.

## Quranic primary passages

- Quran 53:19-23, received wording: https://legacy.quran.com/53/19-23
- Quran 22:52-54, interference, removal and test: https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=22&verse=52
- Quran 17:73-75, attempted pressure to invent alternate revelation: https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=17&verse=73
- Quran 69:44-47, severe warning against attributing false speech to God: https://legacy.quran.com/69/44-47
- Quran 53:26 and 53:62, limits of intercession and chapter's prostration: https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=53&verse=26

The key Arabic distinction is whether tamanna and umniyya concern recitation or wish/desire. The text's statement of removal is not itself a dated account of the gharaniq episode.

## Early transmitted reports and limits

- Al-Tabari, exegesis at 22:52, with several attributed narratives and versions of the gharaniq formula: https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura22-aya52.html
- Al-Tabari, exegesis at 22:53, continuation: https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura22-aya53.html
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1070: https://sunnah.com/bukhari/17/4
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1071: https://sunnah.com/bukhari/17/5

The Bukhari passages report prostration associated with Surat al-Najm but do not quote the alleged Satanic formula. They must not be cited as direct proof of the interpolation. Al-Tabari, who died in 923 CE, is a later recorder, not a seventh-century eyewitness. His traditions include material attributed to earlier transmitters and must be evaluated for transmission dependence.

## Critical modern research

- Shahab Ahmed, Before Orthodoxy: The Satanic Verses in Early Islam (Harvard University Press, 2017): https://nes.princeton.edu/publications/orthodoxy-satanic-verses-early-islam
- Book contents and transmission study: https://books.google.com/books/about/Before_Orthodoxy.html?id=ZCcuDwAAQBAJ
- Patricia Crone, Problems in sura 53, Bulletin of SOAS 78 (2015), 15-23: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-school-of-oriental-and-african-studies/article/abs/problems-in-sura-53/989999016F4B5D5AAE16BE4D338855CA

Ahmed's extensive transmission analysis supports early Muslim circulation and acceptance; it does not provide an independently documented performance of the alleged public utterance. Crone's structural thesis about sura 53 is a different proposal and is not a proof of the gharaniq report. Scholarly abstracts are not falsely represented as full-text inspection.

## Biblical comparison

Deuteronomy 13:1-5, Jeremiah 23:16-32, Galatians 1:8 and 1 John 4:1 establish earlier scriptural tests of religious claims. If an authoritative speaker publicly endorsed pagan intercession as revelation, a Biblical-Christian critique would reject that utterance. This is conditional on the event's occurrence, not independent proof it happened.

## Verdict

[Verified] The canonical text rejects the deities and discusses Satanic interference. [Verified] Early Islamic literary tradition preserved accounts of temporary praise of the deities. [Likely] The story had a substantial early reception history, though extant compilations postdate the alleged event. [Unverified] The historical public utterance and a specific chain of uncontaminated eyewitness testimony. The investigation therefore rejects both the claim that the story is a modern invention and the claim that it is conclusively demonstrated historical fact.
