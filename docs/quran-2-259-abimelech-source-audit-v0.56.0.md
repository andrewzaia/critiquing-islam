# CI-045 - Quran 2:259, Abimelech and the hundred-year resurrection

Original-language, manuscript and Biblical primary-source audit for v0.56.0, 8 November 2026.

## Text-first question

[Quran 2:259](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=2&verse=259) does not name its protagonist or ruined city. Its Arabic directly says God caused the man to die for a hundred years and raised him. It mentions unspoiled food and drink and a donkey, and then describes how bones are put together and covered with flesh. The immediate antecedent makes donkey bones a reasonable contextual inference, but that noun is not repeated with the word bones. [Quran 9:30](https://quran.com/9/30) mentions Uzayr in another setting, not a name identification in 2:259. The [Qira'at comparison](https://quran.com/en/2%3A259/qiraat) distinguishes `nunshizuha` from `nunshiruha` without altering the hundred-year narrative.

## Primary pre-Islamic narrative

The [Greek of 4 Baruch 5:1-3](https://greekdoc.com/DOCUMENTS/pseudepigrapha/4baruch.html) describes **Abimelech sleeping sixty-six years**, not dying and being resurrected. The [Kraft and Purintun translation of 4 Baruch 5:23-35 and 6:8-10](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/publics/pseudepig/ParJer-Eng.html) confirms the unexpectedly fresh figs and their significance as a sign of divine preservation. The Greek passage is a striking literary precursor to the Quran's prolonged temporal dislocation and unchanged provisions near a ruined city. The exact interval, miraculous process, narrative cast and animal differ.

[Jeremiah 38:7-13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah%2038%3A7-13&version=KJV) names Ebed-Melech as Jeremiah's rescuer, and [39:15-18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah%2039%3A15-18&version=KJV) promises his protection. It does not record his extended sleep. Later 4 Baruch expands the character into Abimelech's story. This is the difference between a canonical Biblical person and a later legendary event.

## Date of composition versus surviving manuscripts

- [Jens Herzer, 4 Baruch (2005)](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Herzer.pdf) examines a Jewish origin in the early centuries AD with Christian redaction, textual variants and manuscript groupings. The exact composition date remains debated.
- [Kraft and Purintun manuscript introduction](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/courses/525/ParJer-int.html) documents long-form Greek witnesses including tenth-to-eleventh-century copies and much later translations. A late manuscript can transmit an earlier composition but cannot serve as a first-century autograph.
- [Robert Hoyland (2017)](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/Library/Hoyland%2C%20R%20-%20Rip%20Van%20Winkle.pdf) makes the literary relationship with Quran 2:259 explicit while recognising a proposed, not demonstrated, transmission route.

## The separate Honi tradition

[Babylonian Ta'anit 23a:15-17](https://www.sefaria.org/Taanit.23a.15) narrates Honi sleeping seventy years, a carob tree and a donkey whose offspring have multiplied. The animal is **not** resurrected from a skeleton. [Jerusalem Talmud Ta'anit 3:9](https://www.sefaria.org/Jerusalem_Talmud_Taanit.3.9) transmits a distinct Palestinian Honi narrative. [Moshe Simon-Shoshan (2020)](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/past-continuous-the-yerushalmi%27s-account-of-honis-long-sleep-and-i/) examines how this tradition engages older Abimelech stories; their appearances are not independent eyewitness confirmations of the miracle.

| Demonstration | Divine ability to revive the dead | Preservation, hope of return | Continuity and social dislocation |

## Historical and Biblical verdicts

**[Verified]** The Quran asserts death and resurrection and incorporates fresh provisions and a donkey. The earlier Abimelech story was composed before Islam according to mainstream critical dating and preserves a closely related cluster. The earlier Honi story shares a long interval and donkey motif but not the resurrection of an animal.

**[Likely]** Literary adaptation within Jewish-Christian late-antique narrative circulation is a stronger historically evidenced account of the shared details than a wholly unprecedented Quranic report. The specific person, document or language of transmission remains unproven.

**[Not independently established]** A historical traveller dying for one hundred years and being revived, the animal's bones reassembling, or a direct copying chain. Repeated literary versions are not independent physical witnesses.

**Biblical theological verdict:** Jeremiah and Ezekiel contain related characters and restorative imagery, but no canonical account of the Quranic unnamed century-long death. The later narrative does not acquire Biblical authority merely through Quranic repetition. [John 11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2011%3A38-44&version=KJV) and [1 Corinthians 15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%2015%3A3-8&version=KJV) testify to distinct Christian resurrection claims, not to this event.

**Falsification criteria:** A securely dated independent record of the specific event would alter historicity, whereas a securely dated Arabic/Syriac intermediary preserving the Quran's distinctive details would sharpen literary dependence. Neither is identified here.
