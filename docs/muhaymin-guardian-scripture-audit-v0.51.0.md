# CI-021 - Muhaymin, guardian of earlier scripture, and Quran 10:94
Source-critical audit for version 0.51.0, 8 November 2026.

## Lexical claim: what the original Arabic actually says
[Quran 5:48 in the Quranic Arabic Corpus](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=5&verse=48) describes the Quran as *musaddiqan*, confirming, and *wa-muhayminan alayhi*, and a guardian over it, with an accusative active participle. [Quran 59:23](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=59&verse=23) gives the divine designation *al-Muhaymin*, rendered the Guardian by the Corpus. Guardian is therefore an attested direct lexical gloss, not an optional apologetic invention.

The separate imperative in 5:48 is *fa-uhkum*, so judge. [Quran 5:49](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=5&verse=49) repeats a command to judge by what God revealed. It is important to distinguish the lexical sense of guardian from judicial authority claimed in the surrounding verse. Replacing the standalone word with supreme authority may express a theological reading of the passage but should not be represented as its sole or necessary dictionary meaning.

## Quran 10:94 gives an explicit command
[Quran 10:94 in Arabic and parallel translations](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=10&verse=94) says that if Muhammad is in doubt concerning what was revealed to him, he should ask those who read scripture before him. The [word-level morphological entry for the imperative](https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=%2810%3A94%3A8%29) identifies *fa-s'ali*, then ask, as a second-person singular imperative. This is a direct command within an explicit condition, not a vague suggestion that earlier-scripture readers could theoretically exist.

The conditional does not report that Muhammad actually experienced doubt or conducted an interview. It does direct him, under the expressed condition, towards the scriptural knowledge of earlier readers as a reference point. Calling that only a possible source of verification understates what the Quran commands. The Quran's own appeal creates a direct problem for theories that all earlier-scripture witnesses had become completely unreliable.

## The other Quranic passages are not incidental
- [Quran 5:43](https://quran.com/5/43) says that the Torah possessed by the audience contains God's judgement, while [5:44](https://quran.com/5/44) identifies guidance and light in it.
- [Quran 5:46-47](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=5&verse=47) identifies divine guidance in the Gospel and commands its community to judge by what God revealed in it.
- [Quran 5:68](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=5&verse=68) calls on the People of the Book to uphold Torah and Gospel.
- [Quran 2:41](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=2&verse=41) and [3:3](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=3&verse=3) invoke confirmation of earlier revelation.
- [Quran 2:79](https://quran.com/2/79), [3:78](https://quran.com/3/78) and [5:13-15](https://quran.com/5/13-15) describe particular human abuses involving scripture, but do not independently document an alteration of every Biblical reading.

## Strongest alternative and the burden of proof
An Islamic explanation may distinguish the revelation given to Jesus from later canonical Gospel writings, posit authentic passages mingled with human interpretation, or appeal to the Quran's command to Muhammad to judge by divine revelation. These are different assertions, not direct lexical definitions of guardian. They do not independently establish which Gospel passage was altered, the date of any alteration, or its textual mechanism.

The Quran speaks of the Gospel in the singular and does not name all four canonical Gospels. Therefore the appeal to scripture is not a formal certification of every copy or every sentence in Matthew, Mark, Luke and John. Yet a blanket theory that nothing genuine remained in contemporary Jewish and Christian scripture must explain the Quran's direct appeal to its readers and its commands to judge by earlier revelation.

[Codex Sinaiticus](https://www.codexsinaiticus.org/) and [Codex Vaticanus](https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Vat.gr.1209) constrain the latest possible date of specific extant New Testament readings. They do not alone prove every original utterance of Jesus or disprove every earlier interpolation. A credible corruption hypothesis identifies the disputed text, witnesses, dates, mechanism and reason for preferring a proposed earlier reading.

## Conclusions
**Text-first:** guardian is a primary lexical gloss; the Quran presents earlier scripture as usable divine guidance and 10:94 explicitly directs consultation of its readers if doubt arises.
**Historical:** the claim that a named Gospel teaching was later inserted or suppressed requires independent documentary evidence, not the Quran's asserted power to judge as its own proof.
**Biblical theological:** in the project's Christian framework, the pre-Islamic Gospel witness remains authoritative unless a specific corruption claim survives evidence-based scrutiny; the Quran's own references increase the burden on blanket corruption assertions.

## Falsification
An explicit Quranic declaration that the contemporaneous written Torah and Gospel had no reliable material left would materially weaken the continuity argument. A securely dated manuscript showing a specific changed Christian reading would strengthen that particular corruption claim. Neither a selective modern translation of *muhaymin* nor a retrospective tafsir statement supplies that evidence by itself.
