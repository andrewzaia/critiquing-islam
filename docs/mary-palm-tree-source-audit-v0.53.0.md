# CI-042 - Quran 19:23-26, Mary and the palm tree
Primary-text, manuscript and archaeological audit for v0.53.0, 8 November 2026.

## The historical claim
[Quran 19:22-26](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=19&verse=23) puts Mary beside a date palm at the pains of childbirth. An unnamed speaker calls from below and reports a *sariyyan* beneath her, usually read as a small stream. The feminine imperative *wa-huzzi* orders Mary to shake the trunk so fresh ripe dates fall. Quran 19:26 tells her to eat, drink and keep silent before the people. The next verses culminate in a public statement by her child.

The [Gospel of Pseudo-Matthew, chapters 20-21](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0848.htm), places Mary at a palm during the flight into Egypt, not while delivering Jesus. A child already born instructs the tree to lower its branches and then a water source to open from its roots. The settings, actors, and order differ despite the palm, food and water cluster.

## Original wording and competing interpretations
- [Quran 19:23](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=19&verse=23): *al-makhad* refers to the pains of labour; *jidh al-nakhlah* is the palm trunk.
- [Quran 19:24](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=19&verse=24): the caller is not named in the unexpanded Arabic; *sariyyan* is commonly a stream. A noble-person reading is also discussed in exegetical literature, but is not the primary lexical gloss in this source.
- [Quran 19:25](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=19&verse=25): a direct feminine singular command to shake, *huzzi*, and fresh ripe dates, *rutaban janiyyan*, rather than a child commanding the tree to bend.
- [Quran 19:26](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=19&verse=26): Mary eats and drinks, and is told about silence when she encounters others.

## Earlier Christian narratives and manuscript chronology
- [Brandon W. Hawk's catalogue of Pseudo-Matthew](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-pseudo-matthew/) distinguishes the anonymous Latin work, composition proposals and its varying manuscript families. It is often dated broadly between the later sixth and eighth centuries; a manuscript preserving its opening is not automatic proof that chapters 20-21 were already part of that copy. Hawk's [author explanation](https://brandonwhawk.net/2019/06/29/translating-the-gospel-of-pseudo-matthew/) rejects the text's invented association with Jerome as genuine authorship.
- [Protevangelium of James, chapters 17-19](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0847.htm) places Mary's labour beside a road and her delivery in a cave. This differs from both the Quranic palm and Pseudo-Matthew's flight account. [NASSCAL](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/protevangelium-of-james/) provides critical manuscript context.
- [Stephen J. Shoemaker, Christmas in the Quran](https://islamspring2012.voices.wooster.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/192/2018/10/Shoemaker_christmas-in-the-Quran.pdf) and his [Oxford survey of Dormition traditions](https://academic.oup.com/book/27239/chapter/196834097) document the early Marian Repose textual family, including Ethiopic Liber Requiei and a Georgian witness. Forms of palm, food, water and flight-to-Egypt stories may predate the finished Latin recension. Composition, textual strata, translation and surviving manuscript dates remain distinct.
- [Sozomen, Ecclesiastical History 5.21](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicene_and_Post-Nicene_Fathers%3A_Series_II/Volume_II/Sozomen/Book_V/Chapter_21) gives a fifth-century story of a Persea tree bowing to Christ during the flight. It is not a palm with dates at Mary's labour.

## Pre-Christian parallels without an invented intermediary
- [Homeric Hymn to Apollo, lines 115-122](https://scaife.perseus.org/library/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0013.tlg003.perseus-eng2/) describes Leto grasping a palm tree during the birth of Apollo.
- [Callimachus, Hymn to Delos](https://www.theoi.com/Text/CallimachusHymns2.html), describes Leto near the Inopus stream and a palm at the birth of Apollo.
- [Suleiman A. Mourad, 2002](https://sophia.smith.edu/~smourad/SA%20Mourad--From%20Hellenism%20to%20Christianity%20%26%20Islam%20Origin%20of%20Palm%20Tree%20in%20Quran.pdf), proposes a Greek origin for palm-birth motifs. [A peer-reviewed comparative assessment](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09596410701577332) criticises a direct Greek-myth model that insufficiently considers older Christian Dormition sources. An attested motif is not an independently verified literary transfer into the Quran.

## Kathisma and the post-conquest dating debate
[Rina Avner and colleagues' final Kathisma excavation report](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv2gjwmpj) documents the fifth-century octagonal church near the Jerusalem-Bethlehem route and its associations with Mary's rest late in pregnancy. The later [report by Rina Avner](https://publications.iaa.org.il/cornerstone/vol10/iss1/20/) discusses a date-palm mosaic in the Umayyad layer and suggests possible Quranic associations. This later mosaic cannot be presented as a fifth-century Christian image of the Quranic scene.

Shoemaker argues that the shrine's juxtaposition of birth and palm traditions may imply a post-conquest context for the Quranic narrative. [A 2024 scholarly critique](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jiqsa-2023-0013/html) discusses why the interpretation and dating are disputed. Archaeological continuity of a holy place does not prove when specific Quranic wording was composed or inserted.

## Evidence matrix findings
**Explicit Quranic:** Mary in labour, palm trunk, an unnamed caller, stream, dates, shake command and subsequent public confrontation.
**Explicit Latin:** flight to Egypt, Joseph and the infant Jesus, the bending tree, fruit and water.
**Established earlier motifs:** roadside birth in the Protevangelium, Marian Repose and flight traditions, Greek labour at a palm, and an early responsive tree tradition.
**Independent archaeology:** fifth-century Kathisma church, later Umayyad date-palm mosaic.
**Not proved:** an eyewitness to the miraculous provision, a complete direct manuscript intermediary, verbal copying from Pseudo-Matthew, a precise post-conquest insertion into the Quran, or knowing fabrication.

## Verdict and tests
The Quran's distinctive palm episode lacks independent contemporary corroboration and shares a literary environment with older religious narrative motifs. The similarity is substantial but narrative differences and manuscript chronology block a simple direct-copying claim. A dated pre-Islamic intermediary matching the Quranic sequence, a demonstrably early Kathisma inscription preserving it, or a securely dated Quranic witness missing these verses would materially change specific historical theories. Biblical theology can question an unattested miracle without presenting absence from Matthew and Luke as a mathematical disproof.
