Marcel 11 and 13 are related parts of one codex; do not count them as independent witnesses. Brubaker describes Marcel 21 as composite and dates its first part approximately to the early eighth century. Marcel 7 is dated by him to the ninth or tenth century. Neither date establishes the age of the particular intervention.

## Evaluation

The published correction descriptions are evidence of manuscript intervention. Several of the earlier expressions can retain the identity of the divine agent through context and grammar. The malformed constructions at 24:51 and 35:11 favour copying error, as does the defective parallel construction at 6:40. Sidky's note 32 provides earlier or contemporary witnesses already containing the divine name at five of the loci; this weakens an inference of late invention of the standard wording. The tentative forms at 33:18 and 34:27 are not certain recovered readings.

To establish deliberate theological editing rather than ordinary copying error, locate independent witnesses reproducing a viable earlier expression, date the correction and test the relative frequency of omissions of the divine name against other frequent words. Additional imaging may establish the erased letters. The current evidence rejects universal error-free identity of all early copies, but does not certify either alleged late invention of the standard text or exhaustive prophetic originality.

Next priority: consolidate these located comparisons with the Sanaa, Birmingham, Parisino and Marcel 2 studies in a central catalogue. Retain a separate classification for uncertain reconstructions and distinguish manuscripts from the number of local loci.
