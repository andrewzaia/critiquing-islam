# CI-040 - Paul versus Muhammad: who speaks for God?

Evidence audit, 7 November 2026. Research release v0.43.0.

## Project-wide interpretive standard

Critiquing Islam is an expressly biblical-Christian critique of Islamic claims of divine authority. Biblical statements about Jesus and the gospel form the identified theological criteria, not a concealed claim to religious neutrality. Jesus' divinity is the stipulated Christian theological premise of this comparison, and a genuine commission from the risen Jesus would be divine on that premise.

The study does not label that premise an independently demonstrated historical observation. Document chronology, provenance, independence and textual variants must still be tested with normal historical standards. Neither an ancient believer's testimony nor a later religious text establishes a supernatural encounter without further evidence.

The target is rival religious claims, not Muslims or other people on account of their religious identity.

## Separate questions

1. **Office:** Paul identifies himself as an apostle of Christ, not the seal of the prophets. Quran 33:40 identifies Muhammad as God's messenger and seal of the prophets. The two men did not claim identical titles.
2. **Source priority:** Galatians, Corinthians, Romans and Philippians are the primary Pauline source base. Early Quranic Arabic is the primary source for Muhammad's proclaimed teachings. Later Acts and hadith have different evidentiary status and are identified as such.
3. **Claimed encounters:** Paul claims an appearance of the risen Jesus; the Quran associates Muhammad with revelation and Gabriel, and later Bukhari reports Hira and Waraqa. These are attested claims, not independently verified sightings of supernatural beings.
4. **Biblical criteria:** Deuteronomy 13 and 18, Galatians 1:6-9, 1 John 4:1-3 and 2 Corinthians 11:3-4 provide distinct original-text tests. In context, the older Israelite passages do not explicitly address Muhammad, while Galatians' "different gospel" warning is directly relevant to later Christian interpretation.
5. **Historical consequences:** The crucifixion, Jesus' early religious status, apostolic relations and continuity of the earlier gospel can be tested using sources that predate the Quran.

## The early Christian evidence

Galatians 1:1-24 presents Paul as an apostle commissioned by Jesus and records his claimed conversion. Galatians 1:18-19 reports an encounter with Cephas and James. Galatians 2:1-10 reports a further leadership meeting and recognition of his Gentile mission. Galatians 2:11-14 also describes a genuine dispute with Cephas over Gentile table fellowship. The study does not deny this inconvenient evidence or inflate it into proof that the two leaders agreed on every theological proposition.

First Corinthians 15:1-11 documents Paul describing Christ's death and burial as a received tradition. It also reports resurrection appearances, but each named appearance in Paul's list is not an independent surviving deposition. The historical support for Jesus' execution is stronger than the independent support for the supernatural resurrection event.

First Corinthians 8:6 and Philippians 2:5-11 demonstrate that early Pauline material already contains elevated Christological language and the crucifixion proclamation. The Christian belief in Jesus' divinity is evaluated openly as theology; it is not an archaeological conclusion inferred simply from the manuscript dates.

The consensus category of seven authentic Pauline letters is noted with a limitation: other attributed letters have disputed authorship, and Nina E. Livesey's 2026 study questions the conventional attribution even of the core collection. This is an identifiable minority scholarly challenge, not proof of a new consensus.

Papyrus 46 and fourth-century codices are manuscript witnesses, not Pauline autographs. The earliest physical copy and the date of original writing are distinguished.

## The early Islamic evidence

Quran 33:40 and 3:144 directly establish Muhammad's claimed messenger status. Quran 53:1-18, 81:19-25 and 2:97 provide Quranic material about visions and Gabriel. Bukhari 3 is a much later transmitted Hira account including Khadija and Waraqa, who is described as interpreting the experience rather than observing a heavenly visitor independently.

The Quran's early textual transmission, first Islamic century inscriptions and the Dome of the Rock's dated 72 AH monumental text positively document an early Muslim movement. They defeat a blanket late-invention claim. They do not establish that Gabriel spoke or that the Quran is divinely authored.

## Defects in weak arguments

- It is invalid to argue that Paul had no connection to the first Christians. He reports meetings with Cephas and James. The limitation is that this is his own interested account, not independently signed endorsements of every later claim.
- It is invalid to say Paul and Cephas never disagreed. Galatians 2:11-14 is explicit.
- It is invalid to claim Muhammad existed only in much later legends. Quranic texts, early inscriptions and scholarly study of external notices supply early historical context.
- It is invalid to claim that any miracle report itself certifies divine authority. Deuteronomy 13 makes the source and implications of the message relevant even if an extraordinary sign is asserted.
- It is invalid to assume that Paul's recorded conversion was a scientifically certified divine event simply because the Christian study adopts Jesus' divinity as a premise.
- It is invalid to convert a later attributed Christian statement, a Quranic verse or a hadith into independent eyewitness testimony of the same earlier supernatural event.

## Two explicit verdicts

**Biblical-Christian theological verdict:** Under the stated premise that Jesus is divine and the apostolic gospel is the doctrinal standard, Paul's claimed apostolic mission is consistent with that gospel. Muhammad's message denies core earlier Christian claims about Jesus and the crucifixion and does not meet the Galatians 1:6-9 test for an alternative revelation. The relevant incompatibility has been shown rather than merely assumed from the fact of a claimed angel.

**Historical verdict:** Paul's first-century death proclamation and named contacts with Jesus' earliest movement have materially stronger chronological proximity to Jesus than the seventh-century Quranic correction. The Roman crucifixion is historically well supported. Muhammad's early movement and proclaimed messenger status are also historically attested. No historical method available to this investigation independently verifies the supernatural origin of either man's private reported encounter.

**Falsification criteria:** Earlier, securely dated evidence contradicting the Jesus execution or demonstrating that the original Jerusalem Jesus movement rejected a central Pauline teaching would require revision. Independent evidence of an authentic earlier Jesus prediction of Muhammad, a traceable original Gospel textual variant, or a better early historical witness to a disputed Quranic correction could also affect particular findings. A merely imaginable loss, substitution or additional revelation does not establish any such event.
