# Al-Samiri and the golden calf - source audit v0.27.0

Date: 2026-11-05

## Scope and hierarchy

The Quran is primary evidence for the Quran's narrative wording. The earlier Hebrew Bible is primary evidence for its textual account. Dating and identifying historical actors requires separate linguistic and historical sources, and preservation of a narrative cannot independently establish that the claimed events occurred.

## Direct textual observations

- Quran 20:85 explicitly names al-Samiri as the figure who led the people astray. The longer passage, 20:83-97, assigns the calf's creation to this narrative setting and portrays Aaron opposing the worship. https://quran.com/20/83-97
- Quran 7:148-152, 2:51-54 and 4:153 refer to the calf but omit al-Samiri's name. They are additional passages within one source corpus, not independent Moses-era testimony.
- Exodus 32:1-6 portrays Aaron collecting jewellery, making the image and building the altar. Exodus 32:19-24 and Deuteronomy 9:16-21 preserve an earlier Aaron-centred account. https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.32.1-6?lang=en
- The Quranic Arabic Corpus identifies ya-samiriyyu at 20:95:4 as a proper noun preceded by the vocative particle. It does not by itself provide an ethnic identification or the figure's genealogy. https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=%2820%3A95%3A4%29

## Historical and scholarly comparison

- I Kings 16:24 describes Omri's foundation of the city Samaria. Archaeologist Norma Franklin dates the establishment of the northern capital to approximately 880 BCE. https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/opeds/2013/09/fra378013
- II Kings 17:24-34 describes the region's Assyrian-era resettlement, but should not be treated as an exhaustive or disinterested genealogy of all Samaritans.
- Reinhard Pummer, Samaritans, Oxford Classical Dictionary, describes the long development and differentiation of the Samaritan religious community. Only the public summary was inspected. https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548062125
- Tolga Savas Altinel documents competing proposals for the etymology and identity of Samiri. This demonstrates debate, not proof that any proposed derivation is correct. https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/is/article/1090828
- I Kings 12:25-33 narrates other golden calves in a later northern Israelite setting. Shared images may inform reception history, not prove direct copying.

## Conclusions and limitations

1. The Quran's narrative assigns instigation to al-Samiri and portrays Aaron as an opponent, materially differing from Exodus 32 on the allocation of responsibility.
2. If al-Samiri denotes a later Samaritan identity, the traditional dating of Moses produces a serious chronological objection. The Quranic grammatical form alone does not establish that interpretation.
3. No independently datable Moses-era witness identifying the named Quranic character has been demonstrated by the sources reviewed.
4. A decisive historical resolution would require source provenance, independent linguistic correspondence and chronology, or evidence of an earlier modified version of the Biblical account.
5. Automated scripts verify source presence, citation integrity and basic structural properties, not actual historical events or every form of mobile rendering.
