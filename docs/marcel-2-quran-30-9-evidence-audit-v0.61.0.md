# CI-014 manuscript-level audit - NLR Marcel 2 (v0.61.0)

## Scope and identification

This audit evaluates one located physical alteration, not a conjectural alternative complete Quran. The object is the National Library of Russia's Quran manuscript **Marcel 2** in Saint Petersburg. Corpus Coranicum catalogues it under this exact shelfmark. It must not be confused with Marcel 5, Marcel 9, the Sanaa palimpsest, or a general "Marcel collection". Relevant passage: Quran **30:9**, manuscript **folio 30v**, last line continuing onto the next page.

- Primary witness catalogue: [Corpus Coranicum, NLR Marcel 2](https://corpuscoranicum.org/en/manuscripts/2333/page/1). The publicly accessible index confirms its identity. This release does not claim a new folio-by-folio image collation.
- Modern standard Arabic comparison: [Quran 30:9](https://quran.com/30/9). Relevant segment: `كيف كان عاقبة الذين من قبلهم`. The verse concerns the fate of earlier peoples. This is not an edition of the manuscript's erased wording.
- Daniel Alan Brubaker, *Corrections in Early Quran Manuscripts: Twenty Examples* (2019), Example 4, pp. 44-46, figures 15-16: [published examination and image](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/Library/Brubaker%2C%20D%20-%20Corrections%20in%20Early%20Qur%CA%BE%C4%81n%20Manuscripts.pdf).
- Hythem Sidky, review of Brubaker, *Al-Usur al-Wusta* 27 (2019), pp. 273-288, especially p. 283 and note 25: [review](https://arukiyomi.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Sidky-Review-of-Brubaker.pdf).
- Brubaker, *Corrections to Hythem Sidky's review* (2021): [author's response](https://arukiyomi.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Brubaker-response-to-Sidky-review-of-CEQM-2.3.pdf).

## Date and physical scope

Brubaker reports 42 parchment folios, 20-21 lines per page, script C.Ia, approximately early eighth century on palaeographical grounds, and 26 observed corrections in the manuscript fragment. These are attributed descriptions, not an independently repeated count. No radiocarbon date establishing its ink date is relied upon here. Dating the copy, correction, manuscript parchment, standard codification narrative and later modern research are separate operations.

## What the passage supports

The modern consonantal sequence is `عاقبة الذين` ("the fate of those"). In Brubaker's account, `عاقبة` occurs before a blank erased region at the end of folio 30v; `الذين` begins the next manuscript page. The text **after the correction** locally matches the modern standard. Brubaker estimates the gap might have occupied a connected word approximately four to six letters long. Crucially, he says he **cannot discern the erased wording**. His illustrative possibilities for a former extra expression are expressly not transcriptions of an extant reading.

This establishes a reported physical alteration and a surviving local agreement. It does **not** establish any particular extra word, original semantic variant, doctrine-changing text, or passage omitted by Uthman's committee.

## Competing reconstruction

Sidky proposes **dittography**: the scribe may have written the following `الذين` twice, once at the end of the previous page and once at the start of the next, then removed the redundant first copy. This is plausible because the erased gap is at the page break and the phrase is familiar. Sidky also argues some of Brubaker's hypothetical insertions have incorrect genitive grammar after `عاقبة`.

Brubaker's response challenges Sidky's broad conclusions about scribal correction and distinguishes comparative use of the 1924 Cairo text from alleging that early copyists aimed to conform to a twentieth-century publication. Their larger disagreement does not identify the letters erased at 30:9. A repeated word, an additional lexical reading, or another copyist error remain hypotheses until more physical or independent textual evidence is published.

## Evidence matrix

| Proposition | Status | Required countercheck |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NLR Marcel 2 is an identifiable Quran manuscript | Verified through catalogue | Confirm collection and shelfmark |
| Folio 30v was erased after `عاقبة` | Published examination with photograph | Direct folio inspection, multispectral imaging |
| `الذين` starts the following page | Reported in Brubaker | Full facing-page facsimile |
| The erased word was a known additional reading | Not demonstrated | Legible original strokes or independent early witness |
| The erasure corrected a duplicated `الذين` | Plausible, not proven | Material traces and scribal comparison |
| The correction arose in Uthman's lifetime | Not established | Date of the correction and provenance |
| All Uthmanic codices are forgeries or late inventions | Not established by this locus | Independent codex genealogy and comprehensive textual study |

## Burden of proof and falsification

1. A newly legible under-reading different from `الذين` would strengthen an authentic variant hypothesis.
2. Recoverable repeated `الذين` would strengthen the scribal dittography hypothesis.
3. Independently dated manuscripts with the same extra reading would establish that the form existed beyond this individual page and help test shared ancestry.
4. Secure codicological dating of the intervention would constrain theories about early or late standardisation.
5. The existence of a physical correction is not itself evidence that an alternative recitation was taught by Muhammad.

## Text-first and historical verdicts

**Text-first:** The extant text aligns locally with the standard Quran 30:9. The erased pre-correction wording is unknown.

**Historical:** An early Quranic manuscript contains a published example of scribal erasure. Its cause is disputed, and a standard scribal duplication is a serious competing explanation. This locus by itself does not prove deliberate standardising censorship, and the broader doctrine of exhaustive original preservation is not independently proved by an unreadable erasure either.

**Material-access limitation:** Neither the original object nor its laboratory images was accessed directly during this revision. The distinction between catalogue entries, photographs in published studies, and direct archival work is maintained. No unpublished or copyrighted manuscript photograph was redistributed.
