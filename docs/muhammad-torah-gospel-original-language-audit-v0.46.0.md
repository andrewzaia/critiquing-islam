# CI-011 - Torah and Gospel prophecies about Muhammad
Source-critical audit for v0.46.0, 8 November 2026.

## Precise proposition
Quran 7:157 asserts that earlier-scripture communities find the messenger written or described in the Torah and Gospel with them. The Arabic verbs and participles are checked against the [Quranic Arabic Corpus](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=7&verse=157). A descriptive rather than named fulfilment is logically possible, but must demonstrate identifying specificity rather than generic resemblance.

## Primary textual tests
1. [Deuteronomy 18:15 Hebrew](https://openbible.com/text/deuteronomy/18-15.htm) and [18:18 Hebrew](https://openbible.com/text/deuteronomy/18-18.htm) promise a prophet from the addressed people and their brothers. The immediate context, Horeb reference and Deuteronomy 17:15 favour an Israelite setting.
2. [Deuteronomy 13:1-5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2013%3A1-5&version=KJV) supplies fidelity tests for alleged prophets. These are criteria, not a proper-name prediction.
3. [Deuteronomy 33:2 Hebrew](https://lemma.gebible.app/v/deuteronomy/33/2) describes the Lord's coming from Sinai, Seir and Paran. [Ibn Ezra's medieval reception](https://www.sefaria.org/Ibn_Ezra_on_Deuteronomy.33.2.2) records but does not independently corroborate a three-religions interpretation.
4. Genesis 21:21 genuinely places Ishmael in the wilderness of Paran, while Numbers 10:12 connects Paran with Israel's wilderness itinerary. Neither identifies a future named prophet.
5. [Habakkuk 3:3](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php/verse.php?book=Hab&chapter=3&verse=3) makes God the actor in a theophanic poem. The exact geography of Paran is not certain.
6. [Isaiah 42:11 Hebrew](https://openbible.com/text/isaiah/42-11.htm) calls Kedar's settlements and Sela's inhabitants to praise, but does not identify the servant as a Kedarite or Muhammad.
7. [Isaiah 29:11](https://tips.translation.bible/story/translation-commentary-on-isaiah-2911/) belongs to a sealed-vision illustration, containing both an unable literate reader and an unable illiterate reader, rather than a prophet's first revelation.
8. [Song of Songs 5:16](https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=Sos+5%3A9%2C16) uses Hebrew machamadim as an intensive description of the beloved, not an independently attested prophetic proper name.
9. [John 14:16-26 Greek](https://biblia.com/bible/sblgnt/john/14/16-26) calls the promised Paraclete the Spirit of truth and directly identifies the Paraclete with the Holy Spirit in 14:26.

## Manuscript chronology
The original Torah and prophetic literary settings predate Islam, and fourth-century Vaticanus and Sinaiticus witness to Christian textual transmission before Muhammad. They establish wording, not the factual occurrence of every event or an absolute absence of earlier variation. Individual ancient readings must be checked rather than extrapolating an unlocated original Injil.

## Strongest competing interpretations
The Quran can be interpreted as asserting an indirect description, assigning itself authority to correct earlier scripture, or referring to a now-lost original revelation. Each is logically possible within that theology. Each becomes a historical assertion only when actual earlier words and a credible transmission mechanism are identified. Earlier Christian readings in Acts 3 and Matthew 12 are important reception history, not self-proving supernatural evidence.

## Verdicts
**Text-first verdict:** the proposed Torah and Gospel passages do not name or uniquely identify Muhammad in their original contexts.
**Historical verdict:** the cited pre-Islamic material does not independently corroborate the specific Quran 7:157 prediction.
**Biblical theological verdict:** within the project's declared Christian framework, the proposed identifications do not justify rejecting the earlier Biblical witness.

## Falsification and limits
A securely dated independent pre-Islamic witness providing a distinctive Muhammadan prophecy with verifiable textual history would require reassessment. Alleged lost words, undated interpretations and superficial lexical similarities are not such witnesses. Automated checks verify reference count, navigability and citation use; they do not establish miracle truth, exhaustive manuscript collation or the impossibility of unrecorded speeches.
