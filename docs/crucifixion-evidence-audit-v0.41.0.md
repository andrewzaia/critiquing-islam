# CI-002 - Crucifixion in competing accounts

Source-critical evidence audit for v0.41.0, 7 November 2026.

## Question and burden

The precise dispute is whether Jesus of Nazareth was executed by Roman crucifixion during Pontius Pilate's governorship, and what Quran 4:157 asserts about the people who claimed to have killed or crucified him. A stronger subsidiary allegation is that another man was physically executed instead. The historical standard is the balance of probabilities, not theological certainty. A proposed alternative must identify an earlier source and show more than that the event was conceivable.

## Text-first findings from Quran 4:157-159

The Arabic of Quran 4:157 negates both killing and crucifying using plural verbs directed at the group whose boast is quoted. The phrase *shubbiha lahum* is a form-II passive verb with a recipient pronoun, as analysed by the Quranic Arabic Corpus. The Quran does not name Judas, Simon of Cyrene, a disciple or any other replacement victim. It does not describe the mechanics of a custody switch, physical transformation or eyewitness identification.

The final emphatic denial of killing and the statement that God raised Jesus must be included in interpretation; so must the following 4:159 and related 3:55, 5:117 and 19:33 passages. The dominant traditional interpretation denies Jesus' crucifixion. A narrower interpretation can limit the denial to the group boasting of the act, leaving Roman execution possible; this avoids direct historical conflict but needs an Arabic-contextual defence. It is not the same proposition as a miraculous substitution.

An unwitnessed supernatural rescue is possible as a religious hypothesis. A bare claim of theological authority does not give an independent historical witness to that event.

## What is and is not established

**[Verified]** Paul's first-century letters attest a received death and burial tradition many centuries before the Quran. Its wording is accessible in the Greek New Testament and in later copies including Papyrus 46.

**[Verified]** Tacitus' text identifies Christus as punished under Pilate. His designation of Pilate as *procurator* is a later Roman administrative usage; the contemporaneous inscription identifies a prefect. That distinction improves source criticism, not the substitute hypothesis.

**[Verified]** The earliest extensive canonical Gospel witnesses predate Islam and identify Jesus as the crucified person.

**[Verified]** The idea of a substitute is attested before Islam by Irenaeus' description of the Basilidean view. This disproves an assertion that substitution was first invented by Muslims, but does not establish a first-century substitution event.

**[Verified]** The Quran names no substitute and does not furnish a contemporary Roman or Christian witness to a switch.

**[Likely, strong]** Jesus was crucified under Pilate. The early written material, the Roman report and the political context support this conclusion far more strongly than a later theory of hidden substitution.

**Not established:** The resurrection by the evidence for crucifixion alone; the precise cause of Jesus' death from archaeological remains; every narrated conversation in the Passion accounts; a literal body exchange; or proof of direct borrowing from Basilidean writings into the Quran.

## Three alternative historical models

1. Jesus was executed by Romans under Pilate. This directly fits the earliest Pauline death tradition, Gospel narratives, Tacitus' external notice, the historical governor and known Roman punishment practice.
2. Jesus was crucified but the particular speakers quoted in Quran 4:157 were wrong to boast that they themselves had done it. This can be reconciled with Roman history, but is a contested reading of Quran 4:157's full sequence.
3. An unidentified substitute was crucified while Jesus escaped. This has a pre-Islamic second-century reported precedent, but no comparable early physical or documentary record of the proposed switch and no name supplied in the Quran. Its explanatory burden remains unmet.

The historical verdict rejects the third claim on the balance of probabilities and strongly favours the first. The second, a possible textual interpretation, is not evidence that a substitution occurred.

## Consistency and ethical scope

A claim that every Christian manuscript is identical is not necessary for the argument. The evidence concerns specific texts and their early dates. Manuscripts attest wording, not original events by themselves. Repeated dependent Gospel narratives are not independent eyewitness depositions. Early Roman, Jewish and Christian information should be weighed by independence and proximity, not by the number of later reprints.

The Roman state carried out crucifixion under Pilate. Historical criticism of Gospel blame-shifting must not become collective blame against Jewish people. The study addresses religious texts, evidence and interpretations, not Muslim or Jewish identity.
