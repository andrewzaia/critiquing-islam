# CI-043 - Mary's guardianship and the Protevangelium of James
Primary-text, manuscript and Biblical evidence audit for v0.54.0, 8 November 2026.

## Claim and original wording
The [Quran 3:35](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=3&verse=35) attributes a dedication vow to the wife of Imran, [3:36](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=3&verse=36) identifies the child as Mary, and [3:37](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=3&verse=37) states *wa-kaffalaha zakariyya*, placing her in Zechariah's care. Zechariah visits the *mihrab*, a prayer chamber, and discovers *rizq*, unexpected provision. The Arabic does **not** explicitly say that this chamber was the Jerusalem Temple's Holy of Holies.

[Quran 3:44](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=3&verse=44) calls the account *anba al-ghayb*, news of the unseen, which God reveals to Muhammad. The addressee was not physically present when contenders cast *aqlamahum*, their pens, to determine who would take charge of Mary. Translations sometimes substitute arrows or lots for the functional procedure. The verse neither names the successful candidate nor specifies the date of the competition. A necessary distinction: it is a positive claim of revelation, not merely an obscure possible allusion.

## Earlier primary Christian narrative
[Protevangelium of James, chapters 1-5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0847.htm): Joachim and Anna grieve over childlessness, Anna prays and vows to dedicate a child of either sex. Chapters 6-7: Mary is reared carefully and presented at the Jerusalem Temple aged three. Chapter 8: she is fed by an angel. Chapters 8-9: at approximately twelve she needs another guardian. Widowers bring rods, a priest receives the rods, and a dove emerges from Joseph's rod. Joseph is selected, protests his old age and existing children, then takes Mary to his home.

These parallels are substantial as a cluster: childless mother, vow, Marian holiness, sacred residence, exceptional nourishment and a divinely mediated guardianship. The **differences are textual evidence** too: Anna and Joachim versus the unnamed wife of Imran; a named Temple versus a prayer chamber; an angelic food provider versus God as ultimate source; Joseph versus Zechariah in named caretaker roles; and rods and a dove versus pens and an unspecified outcome. The texts cannot simply be called identical.

The Quran's retrospective 3:44 does not explicitly state that its lot determined the assignment of 3:37, so a proposed two-stage guardianship is *possible*, not proved. Conversely, the Quran does not explicitly describe Joseph's later selection. Neither claim establishes a formal contradiction.

## Manuscript chronology and transmission
- [NASSCAL e-Clavis, Eric M. Vanden Eykel](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/protevangelium-of-james/): mainstream dating places the Protevangelium in the second half of the second century, with Mary's early life patterned on Samuel's childhood.
- [Kimberley A. Fowler, Religions 14 (2023), 636](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/14/5/636): Papyrus Bodmer V is the earliest major Greek manuscript witness, late third century or fourth century. Its surviving title and text must not be confused with the original autograph.
- [Elliott and Rumsey, The Protevangelium of James (2022)](https://www.brepols.net/products/978-2-503-59314-2): critical Greek edition, translation and commentary. Popular English translations do not substitute for collation of variants.
- [Origen, Commentary on Matthew 10.17](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/101610.htm): an early third-century discussion of a Book of James tradition in which Jesus' brothers derive from Joseph's earlier marriage. The passage witnesses a related tradition, not the precise rods and angel-feeding scenes.
- [Paul Foster, The Protevangelium of James (2007)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0014524607081678): highlights the literary Marian theology and low historical confidence of the work. This is critical scholarship rather than an independent eyewitness.

Composition, earliest literary mention, surviving codex and alleged first-century occurrence are four different dates. A dated manuscript authenticates circulation of wording. It does not authenticate Mary's miracles.

## Biblical test
[1 Samuel 1:11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%201%3A11&version=KJV), [1:24-28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%201%3A24-28&version=KJV) and [2:18-21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%202%3A18-21&version=KJV) establish Hannah's vow and Samuel's sanctuary youth. The Protevangelium reuses this available Biblical pattern for Mary. [Luke 1:26-38](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201%3A26-38&version=KJV) and [Matthew 1:18-25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%201%3A18-25&version=KJV) do not report Mary's childhood Temple residence or guardian selection. [Luke 1:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201%3A9&version=KJV) refers to a different lot for priestly incense, not custody of Mary.

Canonical silence does not logically disprove every possibility, but extracanonical Marian biography does not acquire Biblical theological authority merely by reappearing in a later scripture.

## Tested explanations and verdict
**Textual finding:** The Quran's claim of revealed unseen guardianship is explicit; Christian literature with an exceptionally similar Marian childhood narrative antedates Islam by centuries.

**Historical finding:** A literary environment explaining the passage without new revelation exists and is evidenced. A precise Greek-to-Arabic copy chain, a known intermediary and deliberate fabrication by Muhammad are not demonstrated. The guardian, selection and miraculous food lack independent first-century attestation, so the supernatural historical claim does not meet an independently verifiable standard.

**Biblical theological finding:** The canonical Gospels do not affirm the apocryphon's childhood scenes. Their use as factual revealed history in the Quran cannot by itself override the Biblical evidentiary baseline.

**Falsification criteria:** A securely dated independent first-century witness to Mary's Temple residence, a pre-Islamic Arabic or Syriac witness preserving exactly the pens-and-Zechariah episode, or an independently dated earlier non-Christian account of the entire sequence would require re-evaluation. Such material is not established in the listed sources.
