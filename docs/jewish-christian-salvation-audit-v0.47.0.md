# CI-016 - Quranic salvation conditions for Jews and Christians
Original-language and source-critical audit for v0.47.0, 8 November 2026.

## The proposition and its actual burden
Quran 2:62 and 5:69 promise reward to qualifying people explicitly including Jews, Christians and Sabians. Quran 3:85 states that a religion other than Islam will not be accepted. The question is not whether different expressions exist, which is verified, but whether they apply incompatible outcomes to the same people under the same conditions. A real textual tension does not automatically establish a formal logical contradiction.

## Arabic first
1. [Quran 2:62, word-by-word analysis](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=2&verse=62) identifies the conditional *man amana*, belief in God and the Last Day and doing righteous deeds. The verse does not state a time boundary or name Muhammad in its own condition.
2. [Quran 5:69, syntax](https://corpus.quran.com/treebank.jsp?chapter=5&verse=69) repeats that condition. Its nominative *al-sabi'una* contrasts with accusative *al-sabi'ina* in 2:62. The [grammar analysis](https://corpus.quran.com/grammar.jsp?chapter=5&verse=69) explains the syntactic construction; the case variation is not a separate salvation doctrine.
3. Seven parallel [translations of Quran 2:62](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=2&verse=62) and [5:69](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=5&verse=69) show that bracketed qualifiers such as before Muhammad are translator glosses, not words present in the Arabic clause. Interpretive additions must not be quoted as primary source wording.
4. [Quran 3:85, Arabic syntax](https://corpus.quran.com/treebank.jsp?chapter=3&verse=85) rejects seeking *ghayra al-islami dinan*, another religion instead of Islam. [Quran 3:83-85](https://quran.com/3/83-85) gives the immediate context of wider submission and belief in divine revelation.

## Quranic evidence which constrains interpretation
- [Quran 3:67](https://quran.com/3/67) describes Abraham as an upright submitter. Islam has a universal theological sense in the Quran; reading it only as later membership is too narrow.
- [Quran 4:150-152](https://legacy.quran.com/4/150-158) condemns acceptance of some divine messengers while rejecting others. [Quran 7:157-158](https://legacy.quran.com/7/157-158) directs people to follow the unlettered messenger. These provide a specific Quranic basis for a fuller account of acceptable faith.
- [Quran 5:65-73](https://legacy.quran.com/5/48-78) gives a positive assurance to some People of the Book and condemns doctrines relating to Jesus only a few verses later. Any interpretation must explain both, not simply assume one of them was later cancelled.
- [Quran 3:113-115](https://quran.com/3/113-115) praises an upright group among People of the Book. [Quran 3:199](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=3&verse=199) praises people among them who acknowledge revelation sent to both communities.
- [Quran 98:6](https://quran.com/98/6) identifies disbelievers from People of the Book, not necessarily everyone sharing a label. [Quran 22:17](https://quran.com/22/17) attributes final adjudication of named religious groups to God.

## Alternatives and their comparative burden
**Unconditional inclusion:** contradicted by the explicit belief and deeds condition; it is not what 2:62 or 5:69 says.
**Universal exclusion by communal name:** inconsistent with the named positive groups and direct praise of particular People of the Book.
**Earlier-era restriction:** a possible harmonisation documented in later exegesis, but an explicit before-Muhammad boundary is not found within the reward clauses.
**Abrogation:** a distinct historical and textual claim requiring an identified replacement, justified chronological ordering and a reason to exclude contextual restriction.
**Specification through all-messenger faith:** a viable Quran-based reconciliation from 4:150-152 and 7:157-158, although it must explain the shorter wording of the reward clauses and how actual recognition or ignorance is treated.

## Evidence classification and verdicts
**Text-first:** both reward and exclusivity are clearly present, along with independent Quranic constraints on faith.
**Logical:** the direct source evidence does not establish a formal contradiction about identical people under identical conditions. It does refute oversimplified claims of unconditional salvation or absolute exclusion by communal label.
**Historical:** commentators have offered contradictory resolutions. Their statements prove the presence of those interpretations, not the historical truth of an alleged cancellation.
**Biblical theological:** [John 14:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2014%3A6&version=KJV), [Acts 4:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%204%3A12&version=KJV) and [Romans 10:9-13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2010%3A9-13&version=KJV) ground the site's disclosed Christian perspective, while the Quran's internal rules are still assessed on their own wording.

## Falsification and remaining limitations
A clear Quranic clause confining 2:62 or 5:69 to those who lived before Muhammad would strongly support the chronological reading. An unmistakable Quranic example of knowingly rejecting a genuine divine messenger while still qualifying for the reward would challenge the proposed specification. Neither can be replaced by interpretation alone. The study does not identify any actual individual's eternal destiny, and the structural tests do not certify supernatural truth.
