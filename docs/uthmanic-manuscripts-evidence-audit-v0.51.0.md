# CI-014 - Uthmanic standardisation, manuscripts and preservation
Source-critical audit for version 0.51.0, 8 November 2026.

## Precise historical propositions
Three claims must be distinguished. A written archetype was standardised very early. The dominant Quranic consonantal text represents that written archetype with substantial stability. Every original revealed or authorised recitation, including any excluded early forms, is perfectly reproduced by the later official tradition. The material witnesses strongly support the first two, but do not independently establish the third. A narrower claim should not be mistaken for the strongest theological preservation claim.

## Transmitted collection and standardisation narratives
[Sahih al-Bukhari 4986](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4986) attributes collection to Abu Bakr after a crisis involving reciters. [Bukhari 4987](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4987) reports that Uthman ordered a committee to copy Hafsa's written material into official exemplars, specified the language of Quraysh for disagreements, distributed official copies and ordered other materials burned. [Bukhari 4988](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4988) records an attributed search for an al-Ahzab passage. These are substantive reports about an earlier event preserved in later collections, not independently authenticated minutes or original committee documents.

[Reports about multiple authorised recitations](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4992), [Ubayy's disputed material](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5005) and [recitations reported as no longer present](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1452a) require chain and manuscript corroboration. Quran 2:106 contains a replacement principle; that alone does not verify a particular historical recitation's withdrawal.

## Material witnesses and scholarly reconstructions
- [Marijn van Putten, written Uthmanic archetype](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-school-of-oriental-and-african-studies/article/grace-of-god-as-evidence-for-a-written-uthmanic-archetype-the-importance-of-shared-orthographic-idiosyncrasies/23C45AC7BC649A5228E0DA6F6BA15C06), Bulletin of SOAS 82(2), 2019. Shared unusual spellings across fourteen early manuscripts favour copying from a common written model, not a later compilation recreated only from independent oral memories.
- [Hythem Sidky, regional Quranic codices](https://lockwoodonlinejournals.com/index.php/jiqsa/article/view/554), Journal of the International Quranic Studies Association 5(1), 2020, published July 2021. Material and philological comparison reconstructs four regional ancestral branches in the manuscripts analysed, supporting structured official dissemination while identifying later orthographic development.
- [Sadeghi and Goudarzi, Sanaa 1 and the Origins of the Quran](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/san%25C4%2581-1-and-the-origins-of-the-qur%25C4%2581n/), Der Islam 87 (2012), 1-129. The palimpsest's lower text attests a different early Quranic text type and provides positive evidence against an absolute no-alternatives claim. It does not by itself determine the original reading in every disputed location.
- [University of Birmingham, Quran manuscript FAQ](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/facilities/cadbury/birmingham-quran-mingana-collection/birmingham-quran/faqs). The 568-645 CE, 95.4 per cent radiocarbon range dates parchment, not the moment when the ink was applied. The material contains portions of surahs 18-20, not a complete official codex. [Conservation study](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/facilities/cadbury/birmingham-quran-mingana-collection/birmingham-quran/research-and-conservation) found no erased lower text; it is not the Sanaa palimpsest.
- [Shady Hekmat Nasser, Transmission of Variant Readings](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/isbn/9789004241794/html), Brill, 2012. This analyses the subsequent canonisation and transmission of readings. Its critique of claimed tawatur is an attributable scholarly finding, not a seventh-century physical witness.
- [Marijn van Putten, Quranic Arabic](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1163/j.ctv2gjwx0j), Brill, 2022, distinguishes early Hijazi language from later classical reading traditions. [Francois Deroche, The One and the Many](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv270kvh1), Yale University Press, 2021, provides a historical synthesis of early manuscript diversity.

## Evidence standards and distinctions
Consonantal skeleton, orthographic spelling, pointing, vocalisation, later qira'at, oral narratives and lost competing material are not interchangeable categories. Many variants are graphic or phonetic; some may affect words and sense. A manuscript establishes transmitted wording, not direct eyewitness knowledge of what Muhammad uttered. A reconstruction of a common ancestor establishes textual dependency among its descendants, not the content of every unavailable earlier competitor.

## Outcomes
**Established by evidence:** a very early dominant written Quranic tradition; an official-text history consistent with early standardisation; physical evidence of at least one alternative early text type.
**Not independently established:** that every originally taught, authorised recitation was retained without loss, or that the contents of every destroyed codex were identical to the official archetype.
**Not justified:** the opposite exaggeration that Uthman was unknown to Muhammad, wrote the Quran from nothing, or that every early variant proves purposeful corruption.

## What would change the verdict
A securely dated, materially complete and independently authenticated prophetically checked exemplar would materially strengthen the exact-original claim. A secure early witness to a uniquely original reading absent from the official codex, together with independent evidence of that reading's prophetic status, would weaken it. Merely citing a later harmonising or critical tradition does not settle either case. CI scripts check structural coverage, citations and navigation only.
