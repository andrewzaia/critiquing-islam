# CI-008: Mary, Aaron and Imran - evidence audit v0.35.0

## Research scope

CI-008 was expanded from 10 substantive sections and 10 references to 20 sections and 25 numbered references. The historical question is not whether Quran 19:28 includes the words sister of Aaron, or whether Quran 66:12 calls Mary daughter of Imran. Both are direct textual facts. The question is which explanation best accounts for that **combined pattern** when compared with earlier witnesses.

## Quran-first source observations

- Quran 19:16-34 identifies the addressee in 19:28 as the mother of Jesus. The Arabic wording is *ya ukhta Harun*; the original text does not append a parenthetical explanation requiring a tribal or honorific interpretation.
- Quran 3:33-37 narrates the wife of Imran giving birth to a daughter named Mary and dedicating her to God; the family heading and guardianship context must be read with the childbirth scene.
- Quran 66:12 says *Maryam ibnata Imran*, and the Arabic morphology directly identifies the daughter and proper-name construction.
- Quran 20:38-40 and 28:11-12 refer to Moses' sister, but do not give her the name Maryam. Consequently, the Quran does not directly state in one explicit sentence that Jesus' mother is Moses' contemporary biological sister.

These statements were checked against Quranic Arabic Corpus for 19:28 and 66:12 and the Quranic narrative contexts.

## Earlier Hebrew and Greek texts

- Exodus 15:20 in Hebrew names Miriam as the sister of Aaron; Numbers 26:59 names Jochebed as wife of Amram and names Aaron, Moses and Miriam together. 1 Chronicles 6:3 preserves the same Amram family list. These are textual witnesses from the same earlier scriptural tradition, not independent contemporary eyewitness certificates.
- Luke 1:5 says Elizabeth was descended from Aaron. Luke 1:36 identifies her as Mary's female relative using Greek *syngenis*, not as a specified first cousin or as documentary proof of Mary's paternal ancestry. This is relevant counterevidence to a claim that no Aaronic connection could be meant, but it is insufficient to establish Imran as Mary's father.
- The *Protevangelium of James* names Joachim and Anna as Mary's parents and narrates a dedication and temple upbringing. NASSCAL places composition in the second half of the second century CE. It is noncanonical Christian literature, not a contemporary birth register.
- Kimberley A. Fowler, *Religions* 14 (2023) 636, studies Papyrus Bodmer V, the late-third/fourth-century physical manuscript of the Protevangelium. Do not treat composition date, manuscript date and alleged first-century events as interchangeable.

## Earlier symbolic parallels and specialist perspectives

Gregory of Nyssa, *On Virginity* 19, explicitly treats Miriam of Exodus 15:20 as typologically prefiguring Mary the mother of Jesus. This is a genuine pre-Islamic Miriam-Mary parallel, not a new invention of modern apologetics. It is nevertheless not a source for the Quran's claim that Mary's immediate father was Imran.

Nesya Rubinstein-Shemer (2021), *Islam and Christian-Muslim Relations* 32(2), pp. 129-158, identifies a dual Mary overlap and argues it may be deliberate. This summary relies on the institutional publication abstract, not on a claim to have verified every argument in the full article. Nicolai Sinai (2024), *Journal of the International Qur'anic Studies Association* 9(1), assesses Miriam-Mary typology, priestly interpretations and the childbirth context, and notes why Imran cannot simply be dissolved into a symbolic ancestor. The published article text was located through its online version; not every footnote or language witness was independently inspected.

Sahih Muslim 2135 records a reported Najran objection and a response about people reusing prophets' names. It documents the transmitted response, not an independent first-century genealogy or a definitive solution to the two-name cluster. Later tafsir has the same source limitation.

## Evidence-weighted verdict

**Verified textual observation:** the Quran applies the sister-of-Aaron and daughter-of-Imran designations to Jesus' mother, matching a specific earlier Miriam family pattern.

**Strong comparative inference:** engagement with or reuse of the earlier biblical and Christian Mary/Miriam traditions is much better evidenced than a first-century independently attested family identifying Jesus' mother as the daughter of Imran with a contemporary Aaron brother.

**Failed historical corroboration:** the claim that this family grouping is independently established first-century genealogy does not meet the evidence test.

**Still disputed:** whether the overlapping family roles were intentionally typological, accidentally conflated or transmitted through a more complex literary tradition. A typological precedent exists, but neither that nor the earlier Joachim tradition alone establishes the Quranic author's intention or the historicity of Mary's genealogy.

## What could change the verdict?

Securely dated and independent pre-Islamic testimony of Jesus' mother's direct parentage as Imran and the specific Aaron link, or demonstrable literary intermediaries applying the complete two-name formula with a known figurative function, would materially change the historical or literary assessment. A later commentator repeating the same claim is not independent proof.
