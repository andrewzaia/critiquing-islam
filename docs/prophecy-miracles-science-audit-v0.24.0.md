# Prophecy, miracles and celestial claims source audit - v0.24.0

Date: 2026-11-04

## Purpose and source priority

This review strengthens four existing Quran-first investigations. The Quran's wording is evidence of the assertion made within the Quran. The earlier Greek and Hebrew texts are checked on their own terms. Hadith is evidence of attributed narrative traditions, not proof that a supernatural event occurred. Commentary is evidence of proposed readings and cannot supply a lost Greek word, an ancient eyewitness or an astronomical mechanism.

## Direct textual checks

- Quran 61:6, https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=61&verse=6 , explicitly attributes a prediction naming Ahmad to Jesus. This is not itself independent first-century corroboration.
- John 14:16-17 and 14:26, https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2014%3A16-26&version=KJV , describe the Helper, explicitly identifying the Holy Spirit in verse 26. John 15:26 and 16:7-15 continue the description; 1 John 2:1 uses the same Greek role term for Jesus as advocate.
- Philip W. Comfort, The Greek Text of the Gospel of John According to the Early Papyri, New Testament Studies 36 (1990), https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688500019792 , documents extensive early papyrus evidence and its lacunae. A fragmented manuscript cannot prove a particular verse existed unless that portion survives.
- Quran 7:157, https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=7&verse=157 , references Torah and Gospel texts held by an audience. Quran 10:94, https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=10&verse=94 , conditionally appeals to earlier scriptural readers. Neither identifies a now-lost Gospel manuscript or its allegedly removed prophecy.
- Deuteronomy 18:15-18, https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deu%2018%3A15-18&version=KJV , locates a prophet-like-Moses promise in Israelite discourse. Isaiah 42:1-13 names Kedar among those called to praise, not explicitly as the servant's ancestry.
- Quran 54:1-3, https://quran.com/al-qamar/1-8 , asserts the splitting of the moon and records rejection of signs. Quran 29:50-51, https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=29&verse=50 , and Quran 17:90-93, https://quran.com/17/90-93 , describe the response to requests for public demonstrations.
- Quran 67:5, https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=67&verse=5 , associates celestial lamps and projectiles against devils. Quran 37:6-10, 15:16-18 and 72:8-10 contain the repeated eavesdropping and pursuing-fire motif.
- NASA Meteors and Meteorites, https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/ , and NASA's 2026 fireball explanation, https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/watch-the-skies/2026/03/26/its-fireball-season-answering-your-meteor-questions/ , describe meteors as atmospheric phenomena associated with incoming space debris. Those observations cannot verify an invisible supernatural target.

## Main findings and limitations

1. The Ahmad and Paraclete identification fails as a reading of John's actual surviving text. This does not prove Jesus never made another saying.
2. The main earlier-scripture passages proposed to support Quran 7:157 do not explicitly identify Muhammad. A hypothesis about a lost passage needs independent textual evidence.
3. Sources record attributed Muslim witnesses to miracle narratives. Counting later reports or people within a reported crowd does not create independently preserved eyewitness records. The cited evidence does not independently establish the extraordinary events.
4. Meteors have a documented physical mechanism. The Quran's supernatural-pursuit account is not established by astronomy, but ordinary physics cannot logically disprove an unobservable metaphysical claim by itself.
5. A strict contradiction, positive proof of a supernatural event, lack of external corroboration and failure of a claimed scientific prediction are different evidentiary conclusions.

## Automated verification limits

The release checks citation anchors, source-register uniqueness, expected article depth and important source-led findings. Those checks do not certify manuscript folios, eyewitness independence, historical composition dates or the truth or falsity of theological claims. New claims must continue to be assessed against the actual primary sources.
